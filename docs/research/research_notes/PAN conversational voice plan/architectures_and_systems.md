# Architectures and systems for natural, human-like spoken conversation with AI (state as of November 2026)

Scope: Sesame (Maya/Miles), full-duplex end-to-end speech models, production voice systems, Kyutai Unmute, the intelligence vs naturalness trade-off, and inputs for a PAN architecture decision (CPU-only first, then RTX 3050 / 8 GB GPU, then long term). Research done 2026-11-02. "Vendor" = company-reported number; "AA" = independent measurement by Artificial Analysis (leaderboard fetched 2026-11-02; values change over time). Facts from before 2026 are labelled with their date.

## 1. Sesame (Maya/Miles): what makes it feel human, how CSM works, what is open, and what transfers to PAN

### Takeaway
Sesame's human feel comes from a separate "brain" LLM plus a conversation-aware speech generator (CSM). CSM conditions every utterance on about 2 minutes of interleaved text and audio from both speakers, so prosody fits the conversation. Sesame never confirmed which LLM writes Maya's words. The only open weights are still the base CSM-1B (Apache 2.0, CUDA GPU, March 2025), which is much weaker than the fine-tuned demo model. The ideas transfer to PAN better than the weights do: context-conditioned TTS, a consistent personality that pushes back, turn-taking treated as a measured discipline, and a voice character that hands long tasks to background agents.

### Cited Findings
**What Sesame says makes it human ("voice presence", Feb 27 2025 post)**
- Sesame names four ingredients. Emotional intelligence (reading and responding to emotional context). Conversational dynamics (natural timing, pauses, interruptions, emphasis). Contextual awareness (adjusting tone and style to the situation). Consistent personality (a coherent, reliable, appropriate presence). — [Sesame: Crossing the uncanny valley](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- Sesame defines the goal as "the magical quality that makes spoken interactions feel real, understood, and valued." — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)

**How CSM works and uses conversation history**
- CSM is a "single-stage" multimodal model that works directly on RVQ audio tokens. It has two autoregressive Llama-variant transformers split at the zeroth codebook. A backbone reads interleaved text and audio and predicts codebook 0. A much smaller audio decoder predicts the remaining N−1 codebooks with one linear head each. Sesame says the small decoder is what keeps latency low. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- The audio tokenizer is Mimi (Kyutai's split-RVQ codec): one semantic codebook plus N−1 acoustic codebooks at 12.5 Hz. Text uses a Llama tokenizer. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- History mechanism: training samples alternate interleaved text and audio, and speaker identity is encoded in the text. Earlier user audio enters as interleaved audio and transcription tokens. Context length is 2048 tokens, about 2 minutes of audio. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- In the open code, each prior utterance is passed as a `Segment` (transcript, speaker ID, audio), and "context helps the model sound better." — [SesameAILabs/csm](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm)
- Sesame flags a latency problem with standard RVQ delay patterns: N codebooks need N backbone steps before the first audio chunk. Its design avoids this. No latency numbers are published. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- Training data: about 1M hours of mostly English public audio, transcribed, diarized and segmented. Three sizes: Tiny (1B backbone, 100M decoder), Small (3B, 250M), Medium (8B, 300M), trained for 5 epochs. Larger models scored better on Sesame's new benchmarks. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- Evaluation (CMOS, Expresso, 80 raters). Without context, raters showed no preference between CSM and real human speech, so Sesame calls plain naturalness "saturated." With 90 s of prior context, raters consistently preferred the human recording, so a gap remains in conversation-appropriate prosody. Sesame also built homograph benchmarks (lead, bass, tear, wound, row) and pronunciation-consistency benchmarks (route, either, caramel). — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- Sesame's own limitations: mostly English (multilingual "does not perform well yet"). It does not use pretrained LM weights. It models content, not conversation structure such as turn-taking, and Sesame names "fully duplex models" as the future direction. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice); [Sesame research index](https://www.sesame.com/research)

**Which LLM writes Maya's words**
- Sesame's posts do not name it. The open README says only that "a fine-tuned variant of CSM powers the interactive voice demo" and recommends a separate LLM for text. — [SesameAILabs/csm](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm); [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice)
- The Decoder (Mar 2025) reported that the demo voice "reveals that it uses a 27-billion parameter version of" Google's Gemma. It noted this is "not directly stated in the blog post," so it is the author's inference, not confirmed. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sesame-releases-csm-1b-ai-voice-generator-as-open-source/)

**What is open, and whether PAN could use it**
- CSM-1B was released 2025-03-13 under Apache-2.0. It joined Hugging Face Transformers natively in v4.52.1 (2025-05-20). It needs a CUDA GPU (tested on CUDA 12.4/12.6) plus access to the gated CSM-1B and Llama-3.2-1B repos (the Llama repo supplies the tokenizer). Windows needs `triton-windows`. The sample code falls back to MPS or CPU, but support and speed there are not documented. — [SesameAILabs/csm](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm)
- CSM-1B is a base model. It generates audio only, not text. It can produce varied voices but is "not fine-tuned on any specific voice," and non-English quality is poor. — [SesameAILabs/csm](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm)
- No 2026 update appears on the repo. A secondary source on the Oct 2026 launch says csm-1b is still Sesame's only open checkpoint. — [SesameAILabs/csm](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm); [artrealmai summary via search](https://artrealmai.com/article/sesame-october-update-new-voice-model-ios-android)
- Memory: a third-party GPU guide estimates about 3.4 GB at FP16. I found no measured CSM-1B latency or real-time factor on consumer GPUs. — [Spheron CSM-1B GPU guide](https://www.spheron.network/tools/gpu-recommender/sesame/csm-1b/)
- Voice cloning risk: the open model can clone a voice from about 1 minute of audio, and Sesame's safeguards are guidelines only. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sesame-releases-csm-1b-ai-voice-generator-as-open-source/)

**2025–2026 company and product news**
- October 2025: $250M Series B led by Sequoia and Spark Capital, plus a closed beta. Funds go to voice AI and lightweight all-day smart glasses. Co-founded by Oculus co-founder Brendan Iribe. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/22/sesame-raises-250m-advance-lifelike-voice-companions-wearable-devices/); [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/former-oculus-ai-smart-glasses-startup-sesame-raises-250m/)
- October 6 2026 release on iOS and Android with Maya, Miles, Simone and Charlie. Each agent has "its own computer." Users can ask for work (trip planning, research) hands-free and get an update when it is ready. Agents connect to Google apps and user MCP servers and can direct Devin, Claude Code and Codex by voice. — [TestingCatalog, Oct 7 2026](https://www.testingcatalog.com/sesame-updates-voice-agents-with-their-own-computers/)
- The same release brought an updated CSM with "more expressive speech and clearer pronunciation, especially in long conversations." Sesame "reduced sycophancy and repetitive phrasing so characters can push back." Better memory is promised later in 2026, and glasses in 2027 with no exact date. — [TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/sesame-updates-voice-agents-with-their-own-computers/)
- Conflicting status: TestingCatalog calls it a "new preview," while other summaries say the apps left preview for general availability. — [TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/sesame-updates-voice-agents-with-their-own-computers/); [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/sesame-launches-maya-and-miles-as-full-ai-agents-with-their-own-computers)
- TurnBench (Aug 2026, accepted to IEEE SLT 2026): a turn-taking benchmark under the SesameAILabs GitHub, with its leaderboard at turnbench.sesame.com. It scores end-of-turn and interruption detection by recall, false-positive rate and latency on 30 h of triple-annotated two-person conversation across 6 styles. It also releases a 104 h training set. — [arXiv 2608.25218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25218); [GitHub SesameAILabs/turnbench](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/turnbench)
- TurnBench findings. In smooth floor transfers, humans start talking a median 151 ms *before* the other speaker's turn ends. End-of-turn recall is stable across styles. Interruption false positives cluster in backchannel-heavy styles. No current system matches humans without excessive false positives. — [arXiv 2608.25218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25218)
- TurnBench licensing: code is MIT. The data is gated under a non-commercial license that bans voice cloning. — [GitHub SesameAILabs/turnbench](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/turnbench)

### Inferences
- Maya is effectively a hybrid cascade: an LLM writes the words and a large fine-tuned CSM speaks them with conversation context. The "most human-feeling" system is not a monolithic end-to-end duplex model, so PAN's cascaded plan is not the wrong architecture in itself. What PAN lacks is (a) a context-conditioned voice and (b) timing.
- The most transferable Sesame lesson is to condition TTS on the conversation (previous turns from both sides, ideally with audio), not just the sentence. Sesame's own result is that per-sentence naturalness is solved and context-appropriate prosody is the remaining gap.
- Sesame treats "presence" as partly a behaviour and personality problem: anti-sycophancy, pushback, consistent character. PAN can do this today in its fine-tune and prompts at zero hardware cost.
- TurnBench's −151 ms human median means good turn-taking is predictive, not silence-triggered. PAN's 2–3 s first audio is about 15–20x slower than a human floor transfer.
- Sesame's Oct 2026 product matches PAN's direction: a voice companion that hands long jobs to background agents and reports back. Sesame is solving long-task latency through asynchronous delegation, not a faster model.
- CSM-1B for PAN: Apache-2.0 is product-friendly, but the required Llama-3.2-1B tokenizer brings Llama 3.2 Community License terms that should be checked. It is not a CPU option. At an estimated 3.4 GB FP16 it should fit an RTX 3050 8 GB for experiments, but real-time speed there is unmeasured. Out of the box it is a base model that needs a voice prompt or fine-tune and will not sound like Maya, which uses a fine-tuned and probably larger variant.

### Gaps
- Sesame never confirmed the LLM behind Maya. Gemma 27B is a March 2025 inference by The Decoder, and the Oct 2026 release names no model.
- No published Sesame latency numbers for Maya, and no measured CSM-1B real-time factor or first-audio latency on consumer GPUs or CPU.
- Sesame's version and size for the Oct 2026 CSM are not public, and there is no open release beyond CSM-1B. Whether Sesame now runs a full-duplex model in production is not disclosed.
- TurnBench leaderboard values and system names were not retrievable from the repo page.

## 2. Full-duplex and end-to-end speech models: duplex or not, size, hardware, latency, reasoning, voice, license

### Takeaway
Open full-duplex models (Moshi, PersonaPlex, Raon, FLM-Audio, Freeze-Omni, Nemotron VoiceChat) now score well on turn-taking and interruption handling but very poorly on reasoning. AA Big Bench Audio scores run 4–58%, against 90–99% for frontier proprietary voice models. None runs in real time on CPU. The smallest practical setups need a 12–16 GB+ NVIDIA GPU, and most were tested on 24–80 GB cards. The only CPU-targeted options are Liquid's half-duplex LFM3-Audio/Omni-2B and LFM2.5-Audio, which PAN already uses. The 2026 trend is either thinker-talker designs (Qwen3.5-Omni, Step-Audio R1.1, MiniCPM-o 4.5) or a small duplex front-end paired with a separate smart back-end (MoshiRAG, KAME, NVIDIA VoiceChat tool-calling).

### Cited Findings
**Benchmark context (AA leaderboard, fetched 2026-11-02; SR = Speech Reasoning on Big Bench Audio, CD = Conversational Dynamics on a Full-Duplex-Bench subset)**
- Open full-duplex models score: Moshi SR 4%, CD 61.0%. PersonaPlex SR 19%, CD 91.0%. NVIDIA Nemotron VoiceChat SR 27%, CD 52.9%. FLM-Audio SR 16%, CD 62.0%. Freeze-Omni SR 33%, CD 58.7%. KRAFTON Raon SpeechChat SR 58%, CD 86.2%, TTFA 0.04 s. Frontier models such as GPT-Realtime-2 (High), Gemini 3.8 Live and Grok Voice 3.0 score 92–99% SR. — [AA Speech-to-Speech leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech)
- An AA article reported Nemotron VoiceChat at 38.8% on Big Bench Audio, the best open full-duplex model at the time, ahead of Freeze-Omni 31.7%, PersonaPlex 19.1%, FLM-Audio 16.0% and Moshi 4.3%. This conflicts with the 27% now on the leaderboard, probably a different version or date. — [AA article on Nemotron 3 VoiceChat (Mar 2026)](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/nemotron-3-voicechat-leader-speech-pareto); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech)

- The survey puts the L0 (cascade) latency floor at about 500 ms, because "the LLM forward pass and first-chunk TTS dominate once the decision module fires in about 100 ms." MinMo (Alibaba) reports about 600 ms theoretical and 800 ms practical full-duplex latency. — [Survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19453v1)
- Kyutai (Jun 2026) published "Post-training speech models for better interactivity," which uses RL to improve full-duplex dialogue models. The body was not retrievable. — [Kyutai blog index](https://kyutai.org/blog/)

### Inferences
- No open full-duplex model is both smart enough for a personal assistant and runnable on PAN's CPU-only mini PC. Even the best open duplex reasoner on AA (Raon, 58% SR) needs ≥16 GB VRAM.
- On an 8 GB GPU (RTX 3050 or laptop-class), 7–11B duplex models will not run at bf16. Only quantized Moshi-family builds (Candle int8, MLX int4/int8) are plausible, and their weak reasoning means they would only work as a "talker" front-end attached to PAN's own LLM, MoshiRAG/KAME style.
- LFM3-Audio-2B is the only current model aimed at CPU and phone that does speech-in and speech-out with streaming and prefill-while-listening, under a licence workable below $10M revenue. It is the natural "end-to-end, half-duplex" experiment for PAN on CPU, but its real CPU latency is unmeasured.
- Licences that block or complicate a commercial PAN: LLaMA-Omni 2 (academic only), Covo-Audio (research only), Nemotron VoiceChat (possibly research only), and unknown terms for Raon, FLM-Audio, GLM-4-Voice and Step-Audio R1.1. Commercially workable: Moshi (CC-BY 4.0, attribution), PersonaPlex (NVIDIA Open Model License), MiniCPM-o 4.5 (Apache-2.0 per card), Qwen2.5/3-Omni and Step-Audio 2 mini (Apache-2.0), LFM3 (under the $10M threshold).

### Gaps
- No measured CPU real-time numbers for any end-to-end speech model, including LFM3-Audio/Omni via llama.cpp. No official 8 GB VRAM numbers for Moshi int8/int4 or PersonaPlex.
- MiniCPM-o 2.6's licence (earlier MiniCPM releases used a custom model licence) was not re-checked. Only 4.5 was verified.
- VITA-Audio (2025) was not researched in this session; its first-token-latency claims and licence are unverified.

- Raon-SpeechChat licence terms, Step-Audio R1.1 licence, and GLM-4-Voice licence are unverified.

## 3. Production systems: how they achieve natural conversation (native audio vs cascade, turn detection, latency)

### Takeaway
By late 2026 frontier products have converged on three designs. (1) Native speech-to-speech models with selectable thinking levels (OpenAI gpt-realtime-2.x, Gemini 3.x Live, Grok Voice, Qwen Audio 3.0, Step-Audio). (2) Full-duplex voice front-ends that hand reasoning and tools to a backend text agent: OpenAI GPT-Live-1, the clearest example, plus NVIDIA's on-hold-line tool calling and Hume EVI 3's handoff. (3) Voice-vendor cascades tuned end to end (ElevenLabs Eleven Realtime, Deepgram Flux agents, Cartesia Line). The best measured time to first audio is about 0.44–0.62 s. Turning reasoning on pushes it to 1.2–3 s, so the frontier hides "thinking" behind a fast conversational layer.

## 4. Kyutai Unmute: how a cascade is made to feel conversational, and its hardware needs

### Takeaway
Unmute (May 2025, open-sourced July 2025) wraps any text LLM with Kyutai's streaming STT and streaming TTS. Three things make it feel conversational: (1) a semantic VAD inside the STT that predicts end of turn from content and intonation, (2) a "flush trick" that finalises the transcript about 125 ms after end of speech, and (3) a TTS that starts speaking from the LLM's first tokens. It needs a CUDA GPU with ≥16 GB VRAM (x86 Linux/WSL) and gives up the paralinguistic information the LLM never sees.

### Cited Findings
- Architecture: a backend connects STT → any LLM → TTS over websockets. Any OpenAI-compatible LLM server works (e.g. Ollama, OpenRouter). The default local LLM is Gemma 3 1B; production unmute.sh uses GPT-OSS-120B via OpenRouter. Tool calling is not built in. Code is MIT. — [GitHub kyutai-labs/unmute](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/unmute)
- Semantic VAD: the STT model "predicts the text and the probability that the user has finished speaking." The pause-prediction delay adapts to content and intonation instead of a fixed silence wait. It is available only in Kyutai's Rust server. — [Kyutai STT](https://kyutai.org/stt)
- STT models: `kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr` (about 1B, EN/FR, 500 ms delay, semantic VAD built in) and `kyutai/stt-2.6b-en` (English, 2.5 s delay, more accurate). — [Kyutai STT](https://kyutai.org/stt)
- Flush trick: the STT server runs at about 4x real time. When the VAD fires, Unmute pushes audio through fast to cut end-of-speech latency to about 125 ms. On an L40S, the Rust STT server handles 64 concurrent streams at 3x real time. — [Kyutai STT](https://kyutai.org/stt)
- TTS: Kyutai TTS 1.6B (Jul 2025) is conditioned on a 10 s voice sample and can start speaking before the full LLM response is written. Voice cloning access is controlled. — [Kyutai Unmute post (search summary)](https://kyutai.org/2025/05/22/unmute.html); [Kyutai blog index](https://kyutai.org/blog/)
- STT and TTS use Delayed Streams Modeling: a decoder-only model over time-aligned text and audio streams. Delaying text relative to audio gives streaming ASR, and the reverse gives streaming TTS; both handle arbitrarily long sequences. — [arXiv 2509.08753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08753)
- Hardware: CUDA GPU with "at least 16 GB VRAM," x86_64, Linux or WSL (no native Windows or Mac, no ARM). Dockerless per-service VRAM is LLM 6.1 GB (Gemma 3 1B), STT 2.5 GB and TTS 5.3 GB, about 14 GB in total. TTS latency is about 750 ms with everything on one L40S and about 450 ms with services on separate GPUs. — [GitHub unmute](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/unmute)
- Kyutai's stated limitation: because the LLM receives only text, the system loses the user's emotion, emphasis, intonation and tone. — [kyutai.org/unmute](https://kyutai.org/unmute/)
- Kyutai's CPU-class follow-on is Pocket TTS (Jan 2026). It has about 100M parameters, voice cloning from a WAV prompt, about 200 ms to first audio chunk and about 6x real time on 2 cores of an M4 MacBook Air. It runs in Python, as a Rust CPU binary (Linux/macOS/Windows x86), through community ONNX/sherpa-onnx/WASM ports, and as an Android LiteRT port at about 1x real time on a Pixel 8a. Oct 2026 added small English models (18.2M, 6.9M, 1.9M params) at up to 25x real time on one CPU thread; Aug 2026 added training code. Pocket TTS supports 7 languages. Code is MIT, and voice licences vary per voice. — [GitHub pocket-tts](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts); [Kyutai blog index](https://kyutai.org/blog/)

### Inferences
- Unmute's three tricks (content-aware end of turn, fast transcript finalisation, TTS that starts on the first LLM tokens) are the cheapest way to make PAN's cascade feel conversational, and none requires an end-to-end model.
- Unmute itself cannot be deployed on PAN's hardware as shipped (≥16 GB CUDA GPU, Linux), but its pattern ports. PAN already streams TTS on the phone, so the missing pieces are semantic or eager end-of-turn and starting TTS from the first clause.
- Pocket TTS is the most relevant Kyutai artifact for a CPU or phone build (MIT code, CPU-real-time, voice cloning), but it does not take conversation context the way CSM does.

### Gaps
- The model-weight licence for Kyutai STT and TTS was not verified in this session. Moshi's weights are CC-BY 4.0, and it is commonly assumed the same applies.
- No measured end-to-end Unmute latency (user stop → first audio) was found; only component numbers.
- Kyutai STT has no documented CPU path (only PyTorch, Rust/CUDA and MLX).

## 5. Intelligence vs naturalness: how the best designs get both

### Takeaway
Evidence from 2026 is consistent: small end-to-end duplex models are natural but not smart, and smart models are slow when they think. The designs that get both separate a fast "talker" (timing, backchannels, the first words) from a slower "thinker" (text LLM, tools, retrieval). They connect the two asynchronously so the talker's first words cover the thinker's latency. Examples: MoshiRAG's keyword delay, KAME's oracle injection, GPT-Live-1's backend delegation, OpenAI's chat-supervisor, Hume EVI 3's handoff, NVIDIA VoiceChat's on-hold lines. Cascades with a strong text LLM (Unmute, ElevenLabs Agents) are the simpler version of the same idea.

### Cited Findings
- Direct evidence of the trade-off: open full-duplex models reach high conversational dynamics (PersonaPlex CD 91%, Raon 86.2%) but low reasoning (PersonaPlex SR 19%, Moshi 4%). Thinking-enabled frontier models reach 97–99% SR. — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech)
- Same-model trade-off: Gemini 3.1 Flash Live goes from SR 71 at 0.96 s TTFA (Minimal) to SR 97 at 2.99 s (High). — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech)
- Kyutai's diagnosis: full-duplex models handle pauses, interruptions and backchannels, but "factuality remains weak," and scaling them up would make real-time inference too expensive. — [MoshiRAG arXiv 2604.12928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12928)
- MoshiRAG pattern: a full-duplex front end predicts a retrieval trigger token and sends context to a text back end (LLM- or search-based) while dialogue continues. It exploits the "keyword delay" between response onset and the key information, so retrieval finishes unnoticed. Stronger back ends can be swapped in without retraining. — [arXiv 2604.12928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12928); [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/kyutai-s-moshirag-fixes-voice-ai-hallucinations-while-still-talking)
- KAME (Sakana AI, ICASSP 2026): a Moshi-based speech front end answers immediately. A backend LLM (GPT-4.1 in experiments; Claude and Gemini swapped in for demos) is called repeatedly on the growing partial transcript and injects "oracle" signals as they arrive ("speak while thinking"). Training uses simulated oracle sequences. Authors report it significantly outperforms baselines at similar latency. — [arXiv 2510.02327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02327); [Sakana project page](https://pub.sakana.ai/kame/)
- OpenAI GPT-Live-1 delegates reasoning and tool use to a backend agent while staying full duplex. — [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-live-1)
- OpenAI Chat-Supervisor: the voice model handles easy turns and defers hard ones to a text model. The repo says this yields a voice agent that "performs on par with your text agent." — [openai-realtime-agents](https://github.com/openai/openai-realtime-agents)
- Hume EVI 3: the speech model generates the initial response, then the external LLM "take[s] over … as soon as it can." — [Hume EVI 3](https://hume.ai/blog/introducing-evi-3)
- NVIDIA NemotronLabs VoiceChat: tool calls go on a separate output channel while operator-defined "on-hold" lines fill the gap. — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nvidia-opens-nemotronlabs-voicechat-11b-with-tool-calling)
- Thinker-Talker (Qwen Omni family) keeps a text-capable "thinker" LLM and a streaming speech "talker" in one model. Qwen3.5-Omni supports real-time interaction via chunk-wise streaming input and a streaming talker. — [Qwen3.5-Omni blog](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.5-omni); [GitHub Qwen3-Omni](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Omni)
- Inner monologue: Moshi predicts time-aligned text tokens of its own speech before audio tokens, inside a parallel-stream (L2) design. — [Survey 2606.19453](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19453v1); [arXiv 2410.00037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037)
- Reasoning can be trained into speech models directly, but narrowly. Kyutai's Voice of Reason (Sep 2026) used RL plus a speak-while-thinking (STITCH) format to take GLM-4-Voice-9B from 27.3% to 77.1% on spoken GSM8K, while it still performs poorly on TriviaQA. — [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/22/kyutai-releases-voice-of-reason-a-speech-native-model-that-solves-spoken-math-with-reinforcement-learning/)
- Gemini 3.8 Live uses "interleaved reasoning" inside the live model, and Extended Thinking exposes low/medium/high levels. — [Gemini Live capabilities](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/live-api/capabilities)

### Inferences
- For PAN, the "thinker" can stay its fine-tuned LFM3-Omni-2B (or a bigger remote model when needed). The work is in the talker and the orchestration. Speech onset should not wait for the thinker's full answer: start with a short, context-appropriate opener and have the content arrive as it is generated.
- The keyword-delay idea maps cleanly onto a cascade: emit a short natural lead-in quickly (from a tiny fast model, a template, or the LLM's first clause) while the main answer is still being produced. This is the cascade version of what MoshiRAG, KAME, Hume EVI 3 and GPT-Live-1 do.
- Running the thinker speculatively on partial transcripts (KAME; Deepgram's EagerEndOfTurn) is how systems get anticipatory, human-like timing (TurnBench: humans respond about 151 ms before the turn ends). It costs wasted compute when the user keeps talking, which matters on an 8-core CPU.

### Gaps
- No public numbers on how GPT-Live-1's talker and backend coordinate, or on its latency when the backend is invoked.
- No published evaluation of KAME/MoshiRAG-style hybrids on CPU or small GPUs.

## 6. Recommendation inputs for PAN: CPU-only today, RTX 3050 / 8 GB GPU next, long-term target (with licences)

### Takeaway
CPU-only today: keep the cascade but make it streaming, eager and context-aware. That means semantic end-of-turn with speculative LLM start, LLM tokens streamed into phone TTS from the first clause, an instant lead-in to cover thinking time, real barge-in, and conversation-conditioned prosody plus Sesame-style personality work. Optionally test LFM3-Audio-2B's interleaved streaming path. With an 8 GB GPU: move STT, turn detection and TTS to the GPU and try a context-aware expressive TTS such as CSM-1B, but do not expect a good open full-duplex model to fit. Long term (≥16–24 GB hub GPU, or cloud): a hybrid with a duplex talker (Moshi, PersonaPlex or MiniCPM-o 4.5 class) in front of PAN's own thinker LLM, MoshiRAG/KAME/GPT-Live-1 style.

### Cited Findings
**CPU-feasible building blocks (measured or vendor numbers)**
- Smart Turn v3 / v3.1 (Pipecat/Daily): an 8 MB int8 audio turn-detection model. CPU inference takes 9–13 ms on AWS c7a.2xlarge and 57–60 ms on c8g.medium, and Pipecat Cloud uses a v3.2 CPU model by default. ### Inferences
- **CPU-only now (Ryzen 7 5800H, no GPU), most realistic.** A "smart cascade" mirroring Unmute and Deepgram on PAN's existing pieces:
  1. Replace fixed-silence end of turn with a semantic or audio turn model (e.g. Smart Turn on phone or server, BSD-2). Fire an eager or provisional end of turn that starts LFM3 prefill and generation on the partial transcript, and cancel if the user keeps talking.
  2. Stream ASR partials during speech so the LLM prefill is mostly done when the turn ends. LFM3-Audio's causal encoder is designed for exactly this.
  3. Stream LLM tokens and hand the first clause (not the first full sentence) to phone TTS immediately.
  4. Cover thinker latency with an immediate, context-appropriate lead-in or backchannel (the cascade version of keyword delay or EVI 3 handoff), ideally generated by PAN, not canned.
  5. Real barge-in with echo cancellation, keeping only what was actually heard in history.
  6. Sesame-style presence at zero hardware cost: consistent persona, less sycophancy and pushback in the fine-tune, plus prosody control from conversation context (e.g. emotion and pace tags the LLM emits for the TTS).
  Rough expectation: this can move first audio from 2–3 s toward about 0.5–1 s *perceived*. This is an estimate, not measured; the survey's cascade floor is about 500 ms after the decision.
- **CPU-only alternative experiment.** Test LFM3-Audio-2B (or PAN's LFM3-Omni-2B) in interleaved speech-out mode via llama.cpp on the mini PC. It could remove a network hop and the separate ASR stage, but it is half-duplex, voice chat is English-only, and real CPU latency is unmeasured. It also ties PAN's licence to the LFM $10M threshold.
- **With RTX 3050 / 8 GB GPU (opportunistic).** Accelerate STT and turn detection and the LLM, and add a context-conditioned expressive TTS (CSM-1B is the Sesame-lineage option, Apache-2.0; measure RTF first). Keep the cascade with the hub CPU path as fallback, because the GPU is shared and not always available. An open full-duplex E2E model is not realistic on 8 GB with acceptable intelligence.
- **Long term (dedicated ≥16–24 GB GPU in a hub, or cloud).** Hybrid talker+thinker: a full-duplex talker (Moshi/PersonaPlex class, commercial-OK licences, or MiniCPM-o 4.5 if Apache-2.0 holds) for timing, backchannels and interruptions, wired MoshiRAG/KAME-style to PAN's own thinker LLM and agent tools. This is the same shape as OpenAI GPT-Live-1 and Sesame's "agents with their own computer" product. Evaluate turn-taking with Full-Duplex-Bench or a TurnBench-style protocol. TurnBench's own data is non-commercial, so check terms even for internal evaluation.

### Gaps
- No measured end-to-end CPU latency for a full smart cascade on Zen 3 hardware, for LFM3 speech-out on CPU, or for Smart Turn on the phone. PAN would need to benchmark these.
- Whether Kyutai STT/TTS weights are CC-BY 4.0 (and so commercially usable with attribution) was not verified.
- The "0.5–1 s perceived" estimate is an inference from component numbers, not a measured PAN result.
