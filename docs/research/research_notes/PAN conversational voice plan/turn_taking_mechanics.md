# Turn-Taking Mechanics for Voice AI (state as of November 2026)

Notation used below: **[measured]** = number from a benchmark/measurement table in the source (usually vendor-run); **[independent]** = reproduced by a third party; **[vendor claim]** = marketing or self-reported without a published measurement; **[secondary]** = taken from a summary of the original; **[older]** = pre-2025 fact. PAN context: phone (Android) + CPU-only mini PC hub (Ryzen 7 5800H) + later an ESP32-S3 pendant; commercial product, so licenses matter.

## 1. Human baseline: turn-transition timing, overlap, backchannels, turn-end prediction, and which delays feel unnatural

### Takeaway
Humans change turns with a modal gap of about 0 to 200 ms and a cross-language mean near 200 ms; overlap is under 5% of speech; and within-turn pauses are on average *longer* than between-turn gaps, so silence alone cannot separate "pausing" from "done". Listeners predict turn ends mainly from lexico-syntax, with prosody as support; gaps beyond roughly 600 to 700 ms start to carry social meaning (trouble, a dispreferred answer), which is why multi-second AI gaps feel broken.

### Cited Findings
**Timing [older, foundational]**
- Stivers et al. 2009 (PNAS): 10 languages, polar (yes/no) questions, 350 consecutive questions per language across 101 conversations. Overall mode 0 ms; language modes 0 to +200 ms; cross-language median +100 ms; overall mean +208 ms; fastest Japanese (+7 ms mean), slowest Danish (+469 ms), Italian +310 ms; every language's mean lies within ~250 ms of the cross-language mean (about one syllable) — [Stivers et al. 2009, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2705608/)
- Same study: all languages show "a general avoidance of overlapping talk and a minimization of silence between conversational turns"; only 17% of Italian responses overlap — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2705608/)
- Same study, factors that change response speed (regression): nonanswers add ~132 ms; confirmations ~207 ms faster than disconfirmations; responses with a visible component (mostly head nods) ~87 ms faster; questioner gazing at the addressee ~69 ms faster; information requests ~129 ms slower — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2705608/)
- Same study: a 200 ms silence was still judged "on time" in most languages; the subjective "on time" point differs by language (Danish ~203 ms, Lao ~202 ms vs Japanese ~36 ms) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2705608/)
- Heldner & Edlund 2010 as summarized by Levinson & Torreira 2015: ~60% of speaker transitions are gaps and ~40% overlaps; 51–55% of transitions occur in under 200 ms; 70–82% in under 500 ms; true zero gaps (<10 ms) are under 1% **[secondary]** — [Levinson & Torreira 2015, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full); original: [Heldner & Edlund 2010 PDF](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/r.fernandezrovira/teaching/cosp/cosp2016/docs/HeldnerEdlund2010.pdf)
- Switchboard (348 calls, Levinson & Torreira's analysis): 77% of signal is one speaker, 19.2% silence, 3.8% simultaneous speech; between-speaker overlaps are 30.1% of floor transfers, modal duration 96 ms, mean 275 ms; overlap is under 5% of the speech stream — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)
- **Intra-speaker pauses run ~140–150 ms longer than inter-speaker gaps** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)

**How listeners predict turn ends**
- Production latency: single-word picture naming ~600 ms; two-to-three-word utterances ~740–900 ms; sentence planning ~1500 ms. Because gaps are far shorter, next speakers must predict the end of the incoming turn and start planning in overlap, possibly from around the turn midpoint — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)
- De Ruiter et al. 2006: "lexicosyntactic structure is necessary (and possibly sufficient) for accurate end-of-turn projection"; Bögels & Torreira: listeners use final-syllable prosody (boundary tones, final lengthening), especially when lexical cues are ambiguous; a gaze-based turn-yielding cue is "not easy to substantiate" — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)
- Fillers: speakers use "uh" to signal a minor upcoming delay and "um" a major one (Clark & Fox Tree 2002) **[older; from background knowledge, not re-fetched this session]** — [Cognition 84(1)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(02)00017-3)

**Which delays feel unnatural**
- Kendrick & Torreira 2015 (195 responses, telephone corpora): typical preferred and dispreferred responses do not differ much in timing, but for transitions of ~700 ms or more, dispreferred responses clearly outnumber preferred ones — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/116177); [MPI record](https://forms.mpi.nl/node/50430)
- Experimentally, gaps of ≥600 ms generate inferences of an unwelcome response (Roberts et al. 2011); gaps longer than the norm (>300 ms) reduce the likelihood of an unqualified acceptance — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)
- Practitioner target for voice agents: 800 ms voice-to-voice; the same talk puts the human baseline near 500 ms and calls P95 of 800–1000 ms "already concerning" — [AI Engineer talk, Pipecat Cloud](https://ai.engineer/talks/pipecat-cloud-enterprise-voice-agents-built-on-open-source); Pipecat docs: round trip usually 500–800 ms — [Pipecat docs](https://docs.pipecat.ai/)
- Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 authors: human-like repair after an overlap needs response latency of about 1.5 s or less; Gemini Live and Amazon Nova Sonic left 2.0–2.7 s gaps — [FD-Bench v1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23159v3)

**Backchannels (frequency and placement)**
- Switchboard Dialog Act corpus: backchannel/acknowledge is ~19% of all utterances, second only to statements (36%) **[secondary, citing Stolcke et al. 2000]** — [arXiv 1711.05568](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.05568)
- Ward & Tsukahara 2000: a region of low pitch late in the speaker's utterance cues listener backchannels in both English and Japanese — [abstract, J. Pragmatics 32](https://www.cs.utep.edu/nigel/abstracts/jprag00.html). The low-pitch region is described as ~110 ms long — [Ward, Low-pitch regions](https://www.cs.utep.edu/nigel/abstracts/ipo99.html). A secondary summary gives the rule as pitch below ~26th percentile for ≥110 ms, after ≥700 ms of speech, with no backchannel in the preceding 800 ms (English), and notes low predictive accuracy **[secondary, unverified against full text]** — [slide summary](https://speakerdeck.com/yoshikawasan/lun-wen-diao-cha-aidutiyu-ce); the cue works better modelled per speaker because inter-speaker differences cause many false predictions — [Ward & Tsukahara chapter](https://www.cs.utep.edu/nigel/papers/wilks-chapter)

### Inferences
- The realistic AI target is not 200 ms; it is to keep the median user-perceived gap under the ~600–700 ms "trouble" zone and the worst case under ~1.5 s. A cascaded stack that waits a fixed 1.2 s of silence (the sherpa-onnx default, see section 2) before even finishing ASR/LLM/TTS lands well above 2 s, which listeners read as hesitation: this alone explains a "turn-based" feel.
- Because within-turn pauses are on average longer than between-turn gaps, any fixed silence threshold short enough to feel responsive will cut the user off mid-thought, and any threshold long enough to be safe will feel slow. That is exactly the pair of complaints ("cuts me off" and "waits too long"). The fix has to use content (lexico-syntax/semantics) and prosody, as humans do.
- Humans hide production latency by planning before the turn ends; the AI equivalent is speculative ("eager") generation that starts the LLM on a likely end of turn and discards the work if the user continues (see section 3).
- Gaze is unavailable to an audio-only pendant, so lexico-syntax plus prosody are the cues to model.

### Gaps
- No verified per-minute backchannel rate for English conversation was found; only the ~19% utterance share.
- No 2025–2026 controlled user study was found that maps AI response delay to perceived naturalness; the 800 ms figure is a practitioner target.
- Heldner & Edlund percentages come from Levinson & Torreira's summary, not the original tables.

## 2. End-of-turn / endpointing models: what exists as of November 2026

### Takeaway
For a CPU-only, commercial, on-device stack, the only open, permissively licensed, CPU-benchmarked end-of-turn model is **Pipecat Smart Turn v3.2** (audio-only, ~8M params, 8 MB int8 ONNX, BSD-2-Clause, 23 languages, 12.6–94.8 ms measured on server CPUs). Pair it with **Silero VAD v6** (MIT) and a text/semantic check on the hub. LiveKit's detectors have the best published false-cutoff numbers but their license forbids use outside LiveKit Agents; TEN Turn Detection (7B) and Kyutai's semantic VAD (1B, GPU, Rust server) are not CPU-practical; Deepgram Flux, AssemblyAI and Krisp are cloud services or paid SDKs; NVIDIA's Parakeet Realtime EOU 120M (English only, commercial use allowed) is the most interesting hub-side streaming ASR+EOU candidate but has no published CPU numbers.

### Cited Findings

#### VADs (detect speech presence, not turn completion)
- **Silero VAD**: JIT model ~2 MB; "<1 ms" per 30+ ms chunk on one CPU thread **[vendor claim]**; 8 and 16 kHz; trained on corpora covering 6000+ languages; ONNX and JIT, with C++, Rust, Go, Java, C# examples; needs AVX/AVX2/AVX-512/AMX on x86 — [silero-vad README](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad). License: README says MIT, while a badge's alt text says CC BY-NC 4.0; Praat ships it under a "Silero VAD MIT License" page — [README](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad); [Praat manual](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/manual/Silero_VAD_MIT_License.html)
- **Silero VAD v6.0** (release posted 26 Aug; the page omits the year, 2025 by context): 16% fewer errors on noisy real-life data and 11% fewer on multi-domain validation vs v5 **[vendor claim]**; known weak spots are music with voice-like instruments and very high-pitched voices (cartoon/child/artificial); timestamp units changed and `hop_size_ratio` was removed — [v6.0 release notes](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/releases/tag/v6.0). A Habr comparison scores v6 at 0.65 (ESC-50 noise) and 0.53 (private noise) vs v5 at 0.61 and 0.44 (article's own metric) — [Habr](https://habr.com/en/articles/940750)
- **TEN VAD** (Agora/TEN): 16 kHz only; hop of 160 or 256 samples (10/16 ms); library sizes 277–731 KB (Android 373 KB armeabi-v7a, 532 KB arm64-v8a) vs ~2.2 MB for Silero; RTF 0.0086–0.016 on desktop/server CPUs and 0.049–0.057 on low-end Android phones (Galaxy J6+, Oppo A3s) **[measured, vendor-run]**; claims much faster speech-to-silence transitions than Silero ("Silero suffers from a delay of several hundred milliseconds") backed only by a PR-curve image **[vendor claim]**; platforms include Android (C, Java), iOS, WASM, Linux, Windows, macOS; integrated into sherpa-onnx (2025/07) — [ten-vad README](https://github.com/TEN-framework/ten-vad)
- **TEN VAD license**: Apache 2.0 plus additional conditions: you may not deploy it "in a way that competes with Agora's offerings" or enable third parties to develop or deploy applications; deployment must be solely for your own Applications and your direct End Users; commercial use is otherwise permitted — [ten-vad LICENSE](https://github.com/TEN-framework/ten-vad/blob/main/LICENSE)

#### Audio end-of-turn models
- **Pipecat Smart Turn v3.x**: Whisper Tiny encoder layers + linear classifier, ~8M params; int8 static QAT; ONNX; 8 MB int8 CPU build and 32 MB fp32 GPU build; input 16 kHz mono PCM up to 8 s of the current user turn (left-padded; longer input truncated from the start); audio only (no transcript); 23 languages — [smart-turn README](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn); [Smart Turn v3 blog, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.daily.co/blog/announcing-smart-turn-v3-with-cpu-inference-in-just-12ms/)
- Smart Turn v3 latency incl. preprocessing **[measured, vendor-run, Sep 2025]**: AWS c7a.2xlarge 12.6 ms; c8g.2xlarge 15.2 ms; Modal 6 cores 17.7 ms; t3.2xlarge 33.8 ms; c8g.medium (1 vCPU) 59.8 ms; t3.medium 94.8 ms; GPUs 3.3–6.6 ms; CPU preprocessing ~3 ms; v2 was 410–6272 ms on the same CPUs; Pipecat Cloud ~65 ms on a standard 1x instance — [v3 blog](https://www.daily.co/blog/announcing-smart-turn-v3-with-cpu-inference-in-just-12ms/); [README](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn)
- Smart Turn v3 accuracy on the vendor test set **[measured, vendor-run]**: English 94.31% (FP 2.64%, FN 3.06%); best Turkish 97.10%; worst Vietnamese 81.27% (FP 14.84%), Bengali 84.10%, Chinese 88.57%, Arabic 88.60%; fp32 GPU build ~1% more accurate than int8 — [v3 blog](https://www.daily.co/blog/announcing-smart-turn-v3-with-cpu-inference-in-just-12ms/); [README](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn)
- **Smart Turn v3.2** (drop-in for v3.1): single-word replies like "yes"/"okay" misclassified 40% less often on public benchmarks (new short-utterance data + a padding bug fix); cafe and office noise added to train/test sets — [v3.2 blog](https://www.daily.co/blog/smart-turn-v3-2-handling-noisy-environments-and-short-responses/). The README still says it works best with sufficient context and is not designed for very short segments; if the user resumes before inference finishes, re-run on the whole turn — [README](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn)
- Smart Turn license and tunability: code and weights BSD-2-Clause; training data and training scripts published (`train.py`, `train_local.py`, `train_modal.py`, datasets `smart-turn-data-v3.2-train/test` on Hugging Face), so it can be fine-tuned on your own recordings — [README](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn); recommended ONNX Runtime settings: sequential execution, `inter_op_num_threads = 1`, `ORT_ENABLE_ALL` — [v3 blog](https://www.daily.co/blog/announcing-smart-turn-v3-with-cpu-inference-in-just-12ms/)
- Pipecat default stack: `TurnAnalyzerUserTurnStopStrategy` + `LocalSmartTurnAnalyzerV3` + Silero VAD with `stop_secs=0.2` — [Pipecat user turn strategies](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/turn-management/user-turn-strategies)
- **LiveKit audio turn detector** (current default in livekit-agents ≥1.6.1 / @livekit/agents ≥1.4.7): encodes user audio directly (intonation, pitch, rhythm plus meaning), no transcript needed; `v1` runs remotely on LiveKit Inference, `v1-mini` runs locally on CPU in a shared process; 14 languages; with this detector endpointing defaults are `min_delay` 0.3 s / `max_delay` 2.5 s; `v1` falls back to `v1-mini` if no prediction in ~1 s; self-hosted `v1` is billed per request beyond a dev allowance, `v1-mini` is free — [LiveKit turn detector docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector)
- LiveKit Turn Detector v1 keeps an LLM backbone and adds audio encoders; false-cutoff rate at a 300 ms latency budget 9.9% vs Deepgram Flux 12.9% vs ultraVAD 27.7%; 4.5% at a 600 ms budget; released with the open eot-bench (real user turns, English + 13 languages) **[measured, vendor-run]** — [LiveKit blog](https://livekit.com/blog/solving-end-of-turn-detection). An independent reproduction on eot-bench English (400 turns, 705 scored pauses) gives a 95% CI of 7.7–13.8% for the 300 ms figure **[independent]** — [p99lab/turn-1](https://huggingface.co/p99lab/turn-1)
- **LiveKit Model License** (covers the open turn-detector weights incl. `v1-mini`): "You cannot use the LiveKit models on a standalone basis or with any other frameworks"; outputs may not be used to improve non-LiveKit models; derivatives remain "LiveKit Models" — [LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/livekit/turn-detector/blob/main/LICENSE)
- **Krisp Turn Prediction v3** (VIVA 2.0): audio-only, no transcription; multilingual; VIVA models "optimized for on-server CPU deployment"; vendor says it can run on-device (robotics, toys); takes an external VAD flag with each frame; `.kef` model file; requires a Krisp license key; vendor reports 3.5x better turn-taking accuracy **[vendor claim]**; Krisp published `turn-taking-test-v1` (4 h, 976 shift / 1,754 hold cases, 30 speakers) — [Krisp model README](https://krisp-ai-readme.static.hf.space/README.md); [Pipecat Krisp analyzer source](https://reference-server.pipecat.ai/en/latest/_modules/pipecat/audio/turn/krisp_viva_turn.html); [jambonz Krisp docs](https://docs.jambonz.org/self-hosting/krisp); [VIVA 2.0 launch](https://www.01net.it/krisp-launches-viva-2-0-introducing-voice-infrastructure-for-voice-ai-agents/)

#### Text (transcript) end-of-turn models
- **LiveKit text turn detector** (deprecated, slated for removal in Agents SDK 2.0): Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct fine-tune distilled from Qwen2.5-7B; INT8 ONNX; 396 MB on disk, <500 MB RAM; ~50–160 ms per turn on CPU; context up to 6 turns / 128 tokens; predicts probability of the `<|im_end|>` token with per-language thresholds; 14 languages; TPR 99.3–99.4%, TNR from 85.1% (Italian) to 96.3% (Hindi), English 87.0%; v0.4.1 reports 39.23% relative improvement on structured inputs **[measured, vendor-run]** — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/livekit/turn-detector); [LiveKit docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector)
- **TEN Turn Detection**: Qwen2.5-7B-based; three classes finished / wait (user tells the AI not to speak) / unfinished; self-reported English accuracy 90.64% finished, 98.44% unfinished, 91% wait **[self-reported]**; Apache 2.0 "with additional restrictions" — [ten-turn-detection GitHub](https://github.com/ten-framework/ten-turn-detection); [HF](https://huggingface.co/TEN-framework/TEN_Turn_Detection)
- **Namo Turn Detector v1** (VideoSDK): text classifier, ONNX; multilingual mmBERT model (23 languages, ~295 MB, <29 ms) and language-specialized models (~135 MB, <19 ms "after quantization on target hardware"); 90.25% average accuracy multilingual, English 91.5%, Korean 97.3% **[vendor claim]**; license not confirmed in sources found — [VideoSDK blog](https://www.videosdk.live/blog/namo-turn-detection-v1-semantic-turn-detection-for-ai-voice-agents); [dev.to write-up](https://dev.to/sagarkava/namo-turn-detection-v1-semantic-turn-detection-for-ai-voice-agents-4deg)
- **Vogent-Turn-80M**: weights gated under a modified Apache-2.0 license that forbids making it the default turn detector in a horizontal voice-agent platform; no published latency — [community ONNX re-export card](https://huggingface.co/matbee/vogent-turn-80m-onnx)

#### End-of-turn built into streaming ASR
- **NVIDIA parakeet_realtime_eou_120m-v1**: cache-aware FastConformer encoder (17 layers) + RNN-T, 120M params; English only, no punctuation/casing; 16 kHz, ≥160 ms input; 80–160 ms streaming latency; emits an `<EOU>` token at utterance end; EOU latency p50 160 ms, p90 280 ms, p95 320 ms (TTS DialogStudio audio with 3 s silence appended) **[measured, vendor-run]**; avg WER 9.30% at 160 ms on the OpenASR sets; tested on V100/A100/A6000, no CPU notes; no EOU false-positive rate published; **NVIDIA Open Model License, "ready for commercial/non-commercial use"** — [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet_realtime_eou_120m-v1). Community ONNX (CPU/DirectML) and CoreML INT8 ports exist; one ONNX port is labelled CC-BY-4.0, conflicting with NVIDIA's license — [ONNX port](https://huggingface.co/talatapp/parakeet-realtime-eou-120m-onnx-split); [CoreML port](https://huggingface.co/kamilobad/Parakeet-EOU-120M-CoreML-INT8)
- **Kyutai STT `stt-1b-en_fr`** (used by Unmute): ~1B params; English and French; 0.5 s text delay; built-in semantic VAD that outputs the probability the user has finished; the pause-prediction delay adapts to content and intonation; semantic VAD only in the Rust server; weights CC-BY 4.0; trained on 48 H100s, examples load on CUDA, no CPU guidance — [Kyutai STT page](https://kyutai.org/stt); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr); [Unmute announcement](https://kyutai.org/2025/05/22/unmute.html). Third-party docs say Unmute smooths the pause score with an EMA and uses a 0.6 threshold **[unverified]** — [Mintlify mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/kyutai-labs/unmute/architecture/speech-to-text)
- **Deepgram Flux** (cloud): `eot_threshold` 0.5–1.0, default 0.7; `eager_eot_threshold` 0.3–0.9, off unless set, must be ≤ `eot_threshold`; `eot_timeout_ms` 500–60000, default 5000 (silence backstop); events `EagerEndOfTurn` (speculative generation) and `TurnResumed` (cancel); profiles: low-latency eager 0.4 / eot 0.7 / timeout 6000, high-reliability eot 0.85 / timeout 8000; models `flux-general-en` and `flux-general-multi`; only cloud endpoints documented — [Deepgram Flux configuration](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/flux/configuration). A wrapper's docs say eager 0.3–0.5 saves ~150–250 ms at the cost of ~50–70% more LLM calls **[vendor claim]** — [Telnyx end-of-turn docs](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/voice/stt/websocket-streaming/parameters/end-of-turn)
- **AssemblyAI** (cloud): legacy Universal-Streaming used `end_of_turn_confidence_threshold` (default 0.7) plus a min silence when confident (docs disagree: 160 ms vs 400 ms) — [AssemblyAI streaming docs](https://assemblyai.com/docs/streaming); that parameter is deprecated in favour of `min_turn_silence` — [Pipecat AssemblyAI source](https://reference-server.pipecat.ai/en/latest/_modules/pipecat/services/assemblyai/models.html). Universal-3 Pro presets: Fast 100/800 ms, Balanced 100/1000 ms (recommended), Patient 200/2000 ms (entity dictation) — [AssemblyAI voice agent best practices](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/voice-agents/best-practices); `max_turn_silence` defaults 1280 ms (Universal Streaming) and 1536 ms (Universal-3.5 Pro, 768 ms with speaker labels) — [WebSocket spec](https://assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/api-spec/streaming-websocket)

#### Continuous turn-taking prediction: Voice Activity Projection (VAP)
- VAP (Ekstedt & Skantze): stereo input with one speaker per channel (mono is treated as one speaker plus a silent channel); CPC speech encoder + GPT-like transformer with ALiBi; 16 kHz, 50 Hz frames; predicts turn-shift probabilities / future voice activity over a ~2 s horizon; code MIT; training data (Switchboard, Fisher, Candor) not open, so training code is not public — [VoiceActivityProjection GitHub](https://github.com/ErikEkstedt/VoiceActivityProjection)
- Real-time CPU operation shown by shortening the transformer context, "with minimal performance degradation" (Inoue et al., Jan 2024 demo paper) — [arXiv 2401.04868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04868). A multilingual VAP (English, Mandarin, Japanese) matches monolingual models, while monolingual models transfer poorly across languages — [arXiv 2403.06487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06487)
- Third-party ONNX export: ~350–365 ms to process a 20 s window on CPU; labelled CC-BY-4.0 and not endorsed by the authors — [soniqo/VAP-OTO-ONNX](https://huggingface.co/soniqo/VAP-OTO-ONNX). Kyoto's MaAI hosts `vap_en`, `vap_jp` and variants; no license shown in the listing — [maai-kyoto on HF](https://huggingface.co/maai-kyoto/collections)

#### Newer research models (2025–2026)
- **Easy Turn** (ASLP, Sep 2025): acoustic + linguistic (bimodal); four states complete / incomplete / backchannel / wait; 1,145 h training set; claims state of the art over TEN Turn Detection and Smart Turn v2 on its own test set (table excerpt: 96.33 vs Smart Turn v2 78.67) **[self-reported]**; weights "will be made publicly available" — [arXiv 2509.23938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23938); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23938)
- **FastTurn** (Apr 2026, revised Oct 2026): streaming CTC decoding + acoustic features to decide early from partial audio; claims higher accuracy and lower interruption latency than "representative baselines"; released a real-dialogue test set; no weights/license stated — [arXiv 2604.01897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01897)

#### PAN's current endpointer (sherpa-onnx streaming zipformer)
- sherpa-onnx defaults: rule1 = 2.4 s trailing silence even if nothing was decoded; rule2 = 1.2 s trailing silence after some non-blank text was decoded; rule3 = 20 s maximum utterance; `enable_endpoint` defaults to false — [sherpa-onnx C++ API](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/c-api/html/structsherpa__onnx_1_1cxx_1_1OnlineRecognizerConfig.html). Older k2 Python docs described 5 s / 2.0 s / 20 s — [k2 endpointing docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/python/streaming_asr/endpointing.html)

- Expected Smart Turn latency on the 5800H (Zen 3, 8 cores) is probably in the 15–30 ms range, similar to the c7a/Modal rows; on a phone big core, something like the c8g.medium (59.8 ms) to t3.medium (94.8 ms) rows is plausible. Both are guesses that need on-device measurement; either is fine because the model runs only at pauses.
- Parakeet EOU is attractive for the hub because one streaming model would give both partial transcripts and a learned `<EOU>` signal with a commercial-friendly license, but it is English only and has no EOU accuracy or CPU numbers yet.
- If PAN must support languages outside the published lists (Greek, for example, is in neither Smart Turn's 23 nor LiveKit's 14), accuracy is unknown; Smart Turn is the only option here that can be fine-tuned with published training code and data.

### Gaps
- No measured Android latency was found for Smart Turn, Parakeet EOU or VAP; there are no CPU numbers for Parakeet EOU and no published EOU false-positive rate.
- LiveKit v1 model size and internals, and Krisp's model size and latency, are not published.
- OpenAI's `semantic_vad` classifier internals are not documented in the guide fetched.
- Easy Turn weight release, the Namo license and the MaAI/VAP weight licenses could not be confirmed.
- The only cross-vendor benchmark is LiveKit's eot-bench (vendor-run, partly independently reproduced in English); Smart Turn v3.2 does not appear in the LiveKit comparison found.

## 3. Pause handling: not replying mid-thought, and trading false cut-offs against delay

### Takeaway
Production systems split the decision in two: a fast VAD silence (~200 ms) *triggers* a completion check (audio and/or text model), then a short `min_delay` applies when the check says "done" and a long `max_delay` or timeout applies when it says "not done". Extras are speculative "eager" generation, per-session adaptive delays, number/entity-aware waiting, and an explicit "wait" intent. The measured trade-off is steep: LiveKit reports false cut-offs drop from 9.9% to 4.5% when the latency budget goes from 300 to 600 ms.

### Cited Findings
- Pipecat: Silero VAD `stop_secs` default 0.2 s; Smart Turn then judges completion on the whole turn; simpler fallback `SpeechTimeoutUserTurnStopStrategy` with `user_speech_timeout` default 0.6 s; docs warn that a `stop_secs` at or above the STT P99 latency leaves almost no STT wait time and delays turn detection — [Pipecat user turn strategies](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/turn-management/user-turn-strategies); [Pipecat speech input guide](https://docs.pipecat.ai/guides/learn/speech-input)
- LiveKit: fixed endpointing uses `min_delay`/`max_delay`; "dynamic" endpointing (Python only) adapts the delay within that range from the session's pause statistics; user turn limits `max_words`/`max_duration` (off by default) — [LiveKit turns overview](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/build/turns/); defaults 0.3/2.5 s with the audio detector, 0.5/3.0 s otherwise — [LiveKit turn detector docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector)
- Latency vs false cut-off **[measured, vendor-run]**: LiveKit v1 9.9% false cut-offs at 300 ms, 4.5% at 600 ms; Deepgram Flux 12.9% and ultraVAD 27.7% at 300 ms — [LiveKit blog](https://livekit.com/blog/solving-end-of-turn-detection); English CI 7.7–13.8% at 300 ms **[independent]** — [p99lab/turn-1](https://huggingface.co/p99lab/turn-1)
- OpenAI Realtime `semantic_vad`: a classifier scores the probability the user has finished; low probability waits for a timeout, high probability does not; `eagerness` low / medium (`auto`) / high sets the maximum wait; `server_vad` exposes `threshold`, `prefix_padding_ms`, `silence_duration_ms` (example 0.5 / 300 / 500) — [OpenAI Realtime VAD guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-vad)
- Deepgram Flux confidence-based ending with a speculative `EagerEndOfTurn` / `TurnResumed` pair and a 5 s silence backstop — [Deepgram Flux configuration](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/flux/configuration)
- Vapi text-based fallback (`transcriptionEndpointingPlan`): `onPunctuationSeconds` 0.1, `onNoPunctuationSeconds` 1.5, `onNumberSeconds` 0.5; example `waitSeconds` 0.4; "smart endpointing" is English-only — [Vapi speech configuration](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/speech-configuration); [Vapi voice pipeline configuration](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/voice-pipeline-configuration)
- AssemblyAI presets differentiate by use: Patient (200/2000 ms) for entity dictation vs Fast (100/800 ms) for IVR yes/no — [AssemblyAI best practices](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/voice-agents/best-practices)
- Retell: "responsiveness" controls how long the agent waits after it decides the user finished; "dynamic responsiveness" adjusts to the caller's speaking pace — [Retell learn page](https://www.retellai.com/learn/how-ai-voice-agents-handle-interruptions-and-natural-turn-taking); [Retell community](https://community.retellai.com/t/agent-interruption/922)
- Kyutai: the semantic VAD's pause-prediction delay adapts to content and intonation — [Kyutai STT](https://kyutai.org/stt)
- "Wait" as an explicit class: TEN Turn Detection (user tells the AI not to speak) and Easy Turn (complete / incomplete / backchannel / wait) — [TEN Turn Detection](https://github.com/ten-framework/ten-turn-detection); [Easy Turn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23938)
- Smart Turn v3.2 specifically targets short complete answers ("yes", "okay") that older models held open: 40% fewer misclassifications — [v3.2 blog](https://www.daily.co/blog/smart-turn-v3-2-handling-noisy-environments-and-short-responses/)
- Human evidence that silence is a poor cue: intra-speaker pauses are ~140–150 ms longer than inter-speaker gaps — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full); fillers announce upcoming delay — [Clark & Fox Tree 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(02)00017-3)

### Inferences
- PAN's two complaints map directly to a single fixed silence threshold (sherpa-onnx rule2, 1.2 s by default): long enough to feel slow, yet still short enough to cut off long thinking pauses. Replace it with: VAD silence ≥200 ms → audio turn model (Smart Turn) + text check → `min_delay` ~200–300 ms if both say complete, otherwise keep listening up to `max_delay` ~2–2.5 s.
- Cheap text rules worth adding on the hub (Vapi-style): a trailing filler ("um", "uh"), conjunction ("and", "but", "so", "because"), preposition or article means incomplete and extends to `max_delay`; a trailing digit string or entity being dictated also extends (like `onNumberSeconds` and AssemblyAI's Patient preset).
- Per-user adaptation (like LiveKit dynamic endpointing): log the owner's within-turn pause lengths and false cut-offs (the user resumes within ~1 s of PAN starting to talk) and nudge `max_delay` to cover roughly the 90th–95th percentile of within-turn pauses, within fixed bounds.
- An explicit "hold on / wait / let me think" intent should switch to a long timeout (several seconds) until the user resumes; this is where a "wait" class pays off for a personal assistant used for thinking aloud.
- Speculative generation at an "eager" point (e.g. audio p(complete) ≈0.3–0.4 after 200 ms silence) hides LLM latency, but on a CPU-bound hub a discarded speculation competes for the same cores; keep at most one speculation in flight and never start TTS playback before the final commit.

### Gaps
- LiveKit's dynamic endpointing algorithm and its measured benefit are not documented.
- No published numbers were found on how filler- or conjunction-aware rules change false cut-off rates.
- OpenAI's `semantic_vad` timeout per eagerness level is not stated in the guide (commonly repeated 8 s / 4 s / 2 s values could not be verified).

## 4. Barge-in: real interruption vs backchannel, noise or the agent's own echo; recovery; conversation state afterwards

### Takeaway
Production stacks treat user speech during agent playback as a *candidate*, not an interruption: they require a minimum speech duration (~200 ms) and/or a minimum word count, ignore acknowledgement words, and (since March 2026 at LiveKit) use a learned audio classifier on the first few hundred ms of overlap (86% precision / 100% recall at 500 ms overlap, median 216 ms to trigger). On a false interruption they resume after ~1–2 s; after a real one they truncate the assistant's context to what was actually played. No open-weights equivalent of the learned interruption classifier was found.

### Cited Findings
- LiveKit barge-in logic: with a VAD, VAD is the barge-in signal and interim transcripts do not trigger interruptions; final transcripts always do; `min_duration` (minimum speech) and `min_words` (needs STT) filter candidates; a *false interruption* is VAD speech with an empty transcript; `resume_false_interruption` (agent resumes by default) after `false_interruption_timeout` (example 2.0 s); in "adaptive" mode the framework ignores VAD speech shortly after the agent starts talking and re-enables it once a genuine interruption is detected; on interruption the agent "truncates its conversation history to only the portion the user heard" — [LiveKit turns overview](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/build/turns/)
- LiveKit adaptive interruption model: audio encoder + CNN over the first few hundred ms of user speech (waveform shape, onset strength/sharpness, duration, pitch and rhythm); trained on real interruptions, backchannels and other sounds from human conversation with noise mixed in; 86% precision and 100% recall at 500 ms of overlapping speech; rejects 51% of VAD-triggered barge-ins; detects true barge-ins faster than VAD in 64% of cases; ≤30 ms inference; median 216 ms of audio to trigger **[measured, vendor-run]**; GA 19 March 2026 (Python Agents 1.5.0+, TS 1.2.0+); runs in LiveKit Cloud; 40,000 free inference requests/month for development — [LiveKit adaptive interruption blog](https://livekit.com/blog/adaptive-interruption-handling). Docs warn it can mistake a real interruption for a backchannel at the very start or end of an agent turn and expose a `backchannel_boundary` setting — [LiveKit adaptive interruption docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/adaptive-interruption-handling/). A community thread says the model is not open-weights and suggests layering your own backchannel classifier on VAD mode — [LiveKit community](https://community.livekit.io/t/roadmap-for-adaptive-interruption-handling-in-self-hosted-livekit-workers/1172)
- Pipecat: `MinWordsUserTurnStartStrategy` requires N spoken words before a user turn starts (example: 3 words to interrupt the bot) so "okay" doesn't trigger a response; `use_interim` allows earlier detection from interim transcripts — [Pipecat user turn strategies](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/turn-management/user-turn-strategies)
- Vapi `stopSpeakingPlan`: `numWords` (default 0), `voiceSeconds` (default 0.2 s of speech before the assistant stops), `backoffSeconds` (default 1 s before resuming), `acknowledgementPhrases` that never interrupt even when `numWords` is met; recommended start 0 / 0.2 / 1.0 — [Vapi speech configuration](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/speech-configuration); [Vapi voice pipeline configuration](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/voice-pipeline-configuration)
- Retell: `interruption_sensitivity` 0–1; if the agent stops for no reason, lower denoising first; forum suggests 0.8 in noisy environments **[community advice]** — [Retell learn page](https://www.retellai.com/learn/how-ai-voice-agents-handle-interruptions-and-natural-turn-taking); [Retell community](https://community.retellai.com/t/agent-interruption/922)
- OpenAI Realtime: `interrupt_response` and `create_response` flags on turn detection — [OpenAI Realtime VAD guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-vad). `conversation.item.truncate` sets `audio_end_ms` on an assistant item because the server generates audio faster than real time; a value beyond the actual audio length errors — [ConversationItemTruncateEvent type docs](https://v03.api.js.langchain.com/interfaces/_langchain_openai.OpenAIClient.Beta.Realtime.ConversationItemTruncateEvent.html). Developers report the transcript not being trimmed after truncation and truncation landing early when the playback clock and audio timeline disagree **[community reports]** — [OpenAI community: transcript](https://community.openai.com/t/realtime-api-interruptions-dont-properly-trim-the-transcript/1000703); [OpenAI community: Twilio timing](https://community.openai.com/t/openai-realtime-how-to-correctly-truncate-a-live-streaming-conversation-on-speech-interruption-twilio-media-streams/1371637). The Agents SDK now clamps the truncate point to audio the client actually received (PR #4122, merged 2 Aug 2026) — [openai-agents-python PR](https://github.com/openai/openai-agents-python/pull/4122)
- How commercial speech-to-speech systems behave (FD-Bench v1.5): on real interruptions GPT-4o Realtime responded most often (0.78) and stopped fastest (0.23 s); Gemini and Nova Sonic often kept going (Resume 0.55 / 0.71) and took over 2 s to stop; Moshi responded only 0.50. On user backchannels Sonic (0.98) and Gemini (0.93) kept speaking, while GPT-4o stopped within 0.21 s (over-eager) — [FD-Bench v1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23159v3)

### Inferences
- On a speakerphone the barge-in decision must run on the echo-cancelled mic signal; raw VAD will fire on PAN's own voice (section 5).
- Without an open adaptive-interruption model, PAN can approximate one with a layered gate: (1) ≥200–250 ms of speech on the AEC'd signal (Vapi `voiceSeconds` 0.2; LiveKit median 216 ms); (2) duck TTS on onset rather than stop; (3) confirm within ~300–500 ms from streaming ASR partials: two or more words not in a backchannel list ("yeah", "mm-hmm", "uh-huh", "okay", "right", "sure", plus per-language equivalents) means yield, an explicit stop word ("stop", "wait", "no", "hold on") means yield immediately, backchannel-only means restore volume and continue; (4) no words after ~1.5–2 s means a false interruption, so resume from the last sentence boundary.
- Collecting PAN's own labelled overlap clips (backchannel / interruption / cough / TV / echo) would allow training a small onset classifier like LiveKit's (audio encoder + CNN on the first ~500 ms); Smart Turn's published training pipeline is a template for this.
- State after interruption: track the AudioTrack playback position in samples, map it to TTS word or character timestamps, and store the assistant turn as only the words actually played plus an "interrupted" marker, so the LLM does not assume the user heard the rest (OpenAI `audio_end_ms` semantics, clamped to audio actually rendered, as in PR #4122).
- Echo self-check: if the "user" transcript during playback closely matches the TTS text currently playing, discard it as echo.

### Gaps
- No open, CPU-ready interruption/backchannel classifier with published accuracy was found (LiveKit's is cloud-only; Krisp's public model list does not include one).
- OpenAI's exact server-side transcript handling after truncation is not documented in the sources found.

## 5. Echo cancellation for full-duplex on Android and on an ESP32-S3 pendant

### Takeaway
Since Android 10, AEC on `VOICE_COMMUNICATION` capture is only required to be *exposed* (via `AcousticEchoCanceler`) if present, not to be good, and field reports show vendor implementations are broken or missing on some devices. A production assistant needs a software AEC (WebRTC AEC3 is the de facto choice) fed with the exact TTS PCM as reference and a correct render-to-capture delay, plus residual-echo guards in the barge-in logic. On the pendant, Espressif's ESP-SR AFE does AEC with a playback reference channel ("MR" = 1 mic + reference) for roughly 19% of one core in speech-recognition mode, under a license that only allows use on Espressif chips.

### Cited Findings
- AOSP: from Android 10, implementations "should provide" AEC on the `VOICE_COMMUNICATION` capture path, and if provided it "must be discoverable and controllable through" `AcousticEchoCanceler`; default effects per source live in `/vendor/etc/audio_effects.xml` (example enables AEC + NS for voice_communication); NS must not be enabled by default for `VOICE_RECOGNITION`, and effects should be off by default for voice recognition — [AOSP pre-processing guide](https://source.android.com/docs/core/audio/implement-pre-processing)
- `AcousticEchoCanceler` removes the contribution of the signal received from the remote party from the captured audio, attaches to a capture session, and may be inserted by default depending on the `AudioSource` — [Android API reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/audiofx/AcousticEchoCanceler)
- Field reports **[anecdotal]**: hardware AEC "not working properly on all" devices, with headsets as the workaround — [Remotly forum](https://community.remotly.com/threads/problems-with-hardware-acoustic-echo-canceller-on-android-app.121/); Fairphone 2 vendor AEC broken by a missing Qualcomm license, while the Android software fallback "works reasonably well" — [Fairphone forum](https://forum.fairphone.com/t/echo-in-voip-calls/55836/18); /e/ OS echo fixed by disabling hardware AEC and using software AEC — [/e/ issue](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/backlog/-/issues/1510); Vivox notes devices without hardware AEC and toggles between `VOICE_COMMUNICATION`+`STREAM_VOICE` and `VOICE_RECOGNITION`+`STREAM_MEDIA` recording/playback pairs — [Unity Vivox AEC docs](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/vivox-core/developer-guide/android/acoustic-echo-cancellation)
- WebRTC AEC3: a render delay controller estimates the delay between the render (reference) buffer and the capture, with a startup period before it applies changes — [WebRTC source](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src.git/+/47136ddaea7fea55f9d54116f654a8bcf263a6e2/modules/audio_processing/aec3/render_delay_controller.cc). No official standalone Android AEC3 package was found; a community extraction (Kotlin Multiplatform, Android via NDK, MIT, v1.0.2) exposes `setAudioBufferDelay(delayMs)` and ships a loopback demo — [webrtc-aec3-kmp wiki](https://github.com/Enaium/webrtc-aec3-kmp/wiki); [klibs listing](https://klibs.io/project/Enaium/webrtc-aec3-kmp)
- DTLN-aec (neural AEC): MIT license; pretrained TF-Lite model for real-time AEC; 3rd place in Microsoft's AEC Challenge; trained on DNS- and AEC-Challenge data — [DTLN-aec GitHub](https://github.com/breizhn/DTLN-aec); [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.14337). The DTLN family requires per-block execution under 8 ms for real time — [DTLN GitHub](https://github.com/breizhn/DTLN)
- ESP-SR AFE (ESP32-S3): stages AEC (up to two mics), NS, BSS, MISO, VAD (vadnet1_medium), AGC, WakeNet; input channels described by M (mic), R (playback reference), N (unused), e.g. "MMNR", "MNR"; 16 kHz, 16-bit interleaved PCM; `fetch_with_delay()` defaults to 2000 ms — [ESP-SR AFE docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-sr/en/latest/esp32s3/audio_front_end/README.html)
- ESP-SR resource use **[measured, vendor]**: "MR" speech-recognition mode, low-cost: 60.1 KB internal RAM, 739.7 KB PSRAM, 8.8% (feed) + 9.8% (fetch) of one core; MR voice-communication mode: 30.6–32.2% feed + 4.7% fetch; "MMNR" (2 mics + ref): 1.15–1.24 MB PSRAM, 23.7–30.4% feed + 22.9% fetch; WakeNet9 2-ch 3.0 ms per 32 ms frame; no ERLE figures published — [ESP-SR benchmark](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-sr/en/latest/esp32s3/benchmark/README.html)
- ESP-SR license: "ESPRESSIF MIT", granting use only on Espressif products — [Espressif component registry](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp-sr/versions/2.6.0/license)
- SpeexDSP (Xiph) also contains a classic echo canceller under a BSD-style license **[background knowledge, not re-verified this session]** — [speexdsp repo](https://gitlab.xiph.org/xiph/speexdsp)

### Inferences
- When TTS plays on the same phone, the reference is perfect (the PCM written to `AudioTrack`), but the render-to-capture delay (output buffer + DAC + acoustic path + ADC + input buffer) differs per device and per route (speaker, earpiece, Bluetooth). Measure it with a short chirp and cross-correlation at startup and on route changes, seed AEC3's delay with it, and let AEC3's estimator track drift.
- Choose one AEC path per device model rather than stacking: either platform AEC (`VOICE_COMMUNICATION` capture with communication-usage playback, validated per model) or a raw-ish source (`VOICE_RECOGNITION`, which AOSP keeps free of default NS) plus PAN's own AEC3. Double AEC/NS tends to clip speech onsets, which hurts barge-in.
- Small phone speakers at high volume are nonlinear, which leaves residual echo after a linear AEC; cap speakerphone TTS volume, rely on AEC3's suppressor (or a DTLN-aec post-filter if it benchmarks under the 8 ms budget on the phone), and raise the barge-in thresholds while PAN is talking.
- Add a startup ERLE check (play a known clip, measure what remains). If the device cannot reach a safe level, fall back to half-duplex with an explicit barge-in (button, tap, or keyword) instead of shipping self-interruptions.
- For the pendant, cancel echo *on the pendant* where speaker and mic sit together, using the PCM sent to the I2S amplifier as the "R" channel in an "MR" layout. Doing AEC on the phone for pendant audio would add link jitter and clock drift between reference and capture. Budget roughly 19–22% of one core and ~0.75–0.8 MB PSRAM for "MR" speech-recognition mode; the license is fine because the pendant is an ESP32-S3.

### Gaps
- No authoritative cross-device measurement of Android platform AEC quality for voice-assistant use was found, only anecdotes.
- ESP-SR docs don't say whether the reference should come from a hardware loopback or software, and publish no ERLE numbers.
- No Android latency measurement for DTLN-aec was found, and the commercial implications of its training-data licenses were not verified.
- WebRTC's own license was not re-fetched this session (BSD-style, from background knowledge).

## 6. Backchannels produced by the AI

### Takeaway
Of the stacks reviewed, only Retell ships AI backchannels as a product feature, and it is off by default. Open research (VAP fine-tuned for backchannels, NAACL 2025) predicts backchannel timing and type continuously in real time, but no production-ready, permissively licensed backchannel model was found. For a personal assistant, AI backchannels are optional and risky (echo into the mic, the user reading them as PAN taking the floor), so use them rarely and only during long user turns.

### Cited Findings
- Retell: `enable_backchannel` default false; `backchannel_frequency` 0 (never) to 1 (whenever possible), default 0.8; backchannel words customizable per language and voice provider — [Retell interaction configuration](https://docs.retellai.com/agent/interaction-configuration)
- Inoue, Lala, Skantze, Kawahara (NAACL 2025): VAP pre-trained on general dialogue, then fine-tuned on backchannel data; continuous real-time prediction of timing (binary) and type (continuers such as Japanese "un"/"hai", roughly English "yeah"/"right", vs assessments such as "oh"); outperforms baselines on timing and type — [arXiv 2410.15929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15929)
- Classic prosodic cue: low-pitch region (~110 ms) late in the speaker's utterance; low predictive accuracy and strong speaker dependence — [Ward & Tsukahara abstract](https://www.cs.utep.edu/nigel/abstracts/jprag00.html); [chapter](https://www.cs.utep.edu/nigel/papers/wilks-chapter)
- Easy Turn's four-state classifier includes a "backchannel" state (for recognizing user backchannels, not generating them) — [arXiv 2509.23938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23938)
- Humans backchannel a lot: ~19% of Switchboard utterances **[secondary]** — [arXiv 1711.05568](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.05568)

### Inferences
- First choice for PAN: a non-audio "I'm listening" channel (pendant LED or haptic, phone UI) during long user turns. This gives the reassurance of a backchannel with zero risk of echo or false floor-taking.
- If audio backchannels are added: only after the user turn is already long (e.g. >6–8 s); only at a pause of ~200–500 ms where the turn model says "incomplete" (a mid-turn pause) and pitch is low or falling; at most one every ~8–10 s; short, quiet and non-lexical ("mm"); never at a user speech onset. AEC must remove them, or the hub ASR will transcribe "mm-hmm" as user words.
- Retell's default frequency of 0.8 suggests vendors favour frequent backchannels on phone calls, but no evidence about annoyance was found (see Gaps).
- Separately, a short spoken acknowledgement ("okay, checking") when the hub needs more than ~1–1.5 s is a latency mask, not a backchannel, and is probably the higher-value audio cue for a CPU-bound hub.

### Gaps
- No user study was found that quantifies annoyance or benefit from AI backchannels in assistants.
- No permissively licensed, English, ready-to-run backchannel predictor was confirmed (MaAI `vap_en` license unknown).

## 7. Handling user backchannels and side talk while the AI speaks; addressee detection for an always-on wearable

### Takeaway
Commercial speech-to-speech systems still fail here: on FD-Bench v1.5, GPT-4o Realtime stops on backchannels in 0.21 s and responds to 91% of speech aimed at other people, while Gemini and Nova Sonic ignore side talk but are slow to yield to real interruptions. For an always-on wearable, addressee detection needs own-voice/near-field cues plus temporal context. A 2026 edge cascade (SAS) reports audio-only F1 0.86 at 38 ms median on an ARM Cortex-A72, with temporal context as the biggest contributor; these are internal, unaudited numbers.

### Cited Findings
- FD-Bench v1.5 scenarios: user backchannel (99 items, should keep speaking), talking to others (100 items, far-field, should ignore and resume), background speech (100 items, should ignore). Results: talking to others, Gemini 0.99 and Sonic 0.90 ignored it while GPT-4o responded 0.91 of the time; background speech, Sonic resumed 0.98 (responded 0.01), while Gemini (0.70), Freeze-Omni (0.63) and GPT-4o (0.93) often responded inappropriately. Conclusion: fast-yielding systems need better addressee detection and backchannel filtering — [FD-Bench v1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23159v3)
- Backchannel filtering in frameworks: LiveKit adaptive interruption (audio model), Vapi `acknowledgementPhrases`, Pipecat minimum-words strategy — [LiveKit blog](https://livekit.com/blog/adaptive-interruption-handling); [Vapi](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/speech-configuration); [Pipecat](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/turn-management/user-turn-strategies)
- SAS / Selective Attention System (Attention Labs, arXiv 2604.08412, ~Apr 2026): frames addressee detection as a causal decision over current audio plus short history ("Sequential Device-Addressed Routing"); three stages: beamforming (needs ≥2 mics), a 1D-CNN + GRU utterance classifier (~435K params, ~520 KB INT8) on 64-dim log-mels, and a causal transformer (~85K params) over an 8 s window of prior outputs; audio-only F1 0.86 (P 0.89, R 0.83), single-mic audio-only F1 0.84, audio+video 0.95; on ARM Cortex-A72 audio-only median 38 ms, p95 51 ms, under 20 MB; removing the temporal stage drops audio+video F1 to 0.57; TV-active false-trigger rate 7.8% at τ=0.70 vs 3.4% at τ=0.82; F1 below 0.75 beyond four speakers; 600 h proprietary data; all results internal and unaudited — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.08412.md). The paper notes VAD carries no addressee signal and wake phrases also occur in non-directed speech — [same](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.08412.md)
- Apple-style multimodal LLM device-directed speech detection without a trigger phrase (prefix tuning, LoRA) **[older, 2023]** — [arXiv 2312.03632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03632)
- Patents cover detecting whether the *wearer* of an earphone, glasses or watch is the one speaking, so only wearer speech goes to ASR — [US 11488583](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11488583)

### Inferences
- A pendant has a strong cue that room devices lack: the wearer is near-field (louder, higher direct-to-reverberant ratio) while TV and other people are far-field. Combining a near-field energy/ratio feature with a speaker-verification embedding of the enrolled owner gives a cheap "is it the wearer?" prior.
- Policy while PAN speaks: wearer backchannel → continue; non-wearer speech → continue (optionally lower volume); wearer non-backchannel speech → interruption candidate (section 4 gate).
- Policy while PAN is idle: inside a conversation window after PAN's last reply, treat wearer speech as likely addressed; outside it, require a wake word or high addressee confidence. This uses the temporal context that SAS found most valuable.
- For ambiguous segments the hub's 2B LLM can run a text check ("is this addressed to the assistant?") at some latency and CPU cost; keep it off the fast path.

### Gaps
- No open-weights, commercially licensed addressee/device-directed model was found; SAS is a vendor paper on internal data (its SDK is hosted).
- No measured single-mic wearable addressee accuracy exists beyond SAS's internal single-mic figure (F1 0.84) and an unmeasured estimate (~0.60 F1) for a sub-5 MB hearables model.
- Speaker-verification model choices and on-device costs for the owner-voice prior were not researched in this pass.

## 8. Overlap and cooperative interruptions (continue, adapt, or yield)

### Takeaway
2025–2026 research reframes overlap handling from a binary "stop or keep talking" to three outcomes: continue unchanged, adapt within the turn (absorb a correction, completion or clarification), or yield. Humans adapt in 68.2% of collaborative overlaps versus 34.8% for the full-duplex model tested (PersonaPlex), and a separate 2026 benchmark finds most full-duplex systems biased toward yielding. These full-duplex models are GPU-class; a CPU cascade can approximate "adapt" by classifying the overlapping words and regenerating the rest of the response.

### Cited Findings
- "Continue, Adapt, or Yield: In-Turn Adaptation to Overlapping Speech in Full-Duplex Agents" (Lu et al., arXiv 2609.13117, 11 Sep 2026; NeurIPS 2026 listing): Duplex Cue evaluation separates listener intent (backchannel, collaboration, interruption) from speaker response (continue, adapt, yield); 300 human-confirmed cues from unscripted English conversation, 208 scorable pairs; on 66 collaboration pairs humans adapt 68.2% vs PersonaPlex 34.8% (PersonaPlex continues unchanged 42.4%, yields 22.7%); single-model case study, preliminary — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13117); [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/162429)
- "Same Words, Different Actions: Paired Turn-Taking Evaluation under Rewritten Dialogue Contexts" (ECHO, arXiv 2609.17360) reportedly finds most full-duplex systems show a pronounced bias toward Yield **[from search summary, not full text]** — [arXiv 2609.17360](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17360)
- Instruct-FD (arXiv 2607.20460) tests whether full-duplex systems follow turn-taking instructions (title only reviewed) — [arXiv 2607.20460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20460)
- FD-Bench v1.5: a trade-off between fast yielding and robust floor-holding; on re-entry after overlap GPT-4o (and to a lesser degree Gemini) sped up and raised pitch, while Moshi changed abruptly ("brittle control"); recommendation that prosodic adjustment on re-entry be controlled, not abrupt — [FD-Bench v1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23159v3)
- Human overlaps are mostly brief: between-speaker overlaps are 30.1% of floor transfers in Switchboard, modal 96 ms, mean 275 ms — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)
- Full-duplex/semantic-VAD models of this generation are GPU-trained and GPU-served (e.g. Kyutai STT 1B trained on 48 H100s, examples on CUDA) — [Kyutai HF card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr)

### Inferences
- A CPU cascade can emulate the three-way policy: when the overlap gate (section 4) fires, classify the overlapping words on the hub into backchannel / collaborative (correction, completion, clarification) / interruption (stop, new request) / side talk, then continue / adapt (brief acknowledgement such as "right, Thursday", then regenerate the *remaining* response with the correction) / yield (stop and answer) / ignore.
- Keep "yield" fast (stop within ~200–300 ms) for imperative stop words and clear new requests (GPT-4o's strength), but don't yield to every overlap (GPT-4o's failure on backchannels and side talk), and never hold the floor for 2+ s on a clear interruption (Gemini/Sonic's failure).
- On re-entry after an adapt or resume, keep TTS prosody steady rather than speeding up, per FD-Bench's observation that abrupt changes read as brittle.

### Gaps
- The CAY abstract reports only collaboration-pair results; no backchannel or interruption breakdown and no released model.
- No CPU-feasible full-duplex speech model was found.

## 9. Concrete design for a CPU-only phone + mini PC (and later pendant)

### Takeaway
Put cheap, latency-critical signal processing where the microphone is (phone now, pendant later): AEC → VAD → audio turn model (Smart Turn) → barge-in gate. Put the semantic layer on the hub: streaming ASR fed continuously (not after the endpoint), a text-completeness check, speculative LLM start, overlap classification and per-user adaptation. Fuse audio and text: a short `min_delay` (~200–300 ms) when both say complete, a `max_delay` (~2–2.5 s) when either says incomplete, a long timeout on explicit "wait". Target a median voice-to-voice latency of 800 ms or less and keep worst cases under ~1.5 s.

### Cited Findings (parameter anchors used below)
- VAD silence trigger 0.2 s (Pipecat `stop_secs`); timeout-only fallback 0.6 s — [Pipecat](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/turn-management/user-turn-strategies)
- `min_delay`/`max_delay` 0.3/2.5 s with an audio turn model, 0.5/3.0 s with a text model — [LiveKit turn detector docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector)
- Balanced 100/1000 ms and Patient 200/2000 ms silence presets — [AssemblyAI best practices](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/voice-agents/best-practices)
- Eager 0.4 / final 0.7 / timeout 6 s low-latency profile — [Deepgram Flux configuration](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/flux/configuration)
- Barge-in: 0.2 s of speech (`voiceSeconds`), resume back-off 1 s — [Vapi](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/speech-configuration); false-interruption resume timeout example 2.0 s — [LiveKit turns overview](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/build/turns/); learned interrupt decision median 216 ms — [LiveKit adaptive interruption blog](https://livekit.com/blog/adaptive-interruption-handling)
- Smart Turn input ≤8 s of the current turn, ORT sequential with 1 inter-op thread — [README](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn); [v3 blog](https://www.daily.co/blog/announcing-smart-turn-v3-with-cpu-inference-in-just-12ms/)
- ESP-SR "MR" AEC ~18.6–19.1% of one core in SR mode — [ESP-SR benchmark](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-sr/en/latest/esp32s3/benchmark/README.html)
- 800 ms voice-to-voice target — [AI Engineer talk](https://ai.engineer/talks/pipecat-cloud-enterprise-voice-agents-built-on-open-source)

### Inferences
**Phone (now)**
1. Capture 16 kHz mono. Per device model, pick one AEC path (validated platform AEC, or `VOICE_RECOGNITION` + WebRTC AEC3 with the TTS PCM as reference and a chirp-measured delay); run an ERLE self-test and fall back to half-duplex with tap/keyword barge-in if it fails.
2. Silero VAD v6 (MIT) on the AEC'd signal, ~32 ms frames. User speech onset: ≥200–250 ms of speech; keep ~300 ms of pre-roll.
3. Stream AEC'd audio to the hub continuously while the user speaks, so hub ASR is already caught up when the turn ends; endpoint decisions then add no upload time.
4. At each VAD silence ≥200 ms, run Smart Turn v3.2 int8 on the last ≤8 s of the user's audio. Run it on the phone if measured latency is under ~100 ms, otherwise on the hub (probably ~15–30 ms, unmeasured).
5. Barge-in gate during playback: speech ≥200–250 ms on the AEC'd signal → duck TTS → confirm within ~300–500 ms from hub ASR partials (≥2 non-backchannel words, or a stop word) → yield; backchannel-only → restore and continue; no words in ~1.5–2 s → resume from the last sentence boundary. Ignore VAD in the first ~200–300 ms after PAN starts a sentence unless the onset is strong, as LiveKit's adaptive mode gates VAD right after the agent starts talking.
6. Track the played sample position for exact truncation of the assistant turn.

**Hub (Ryzen 7 5800H, CPU)**
1. Streaming ASR on the continuous stream (current model, or benchmark Parakeet Realtime EOU 120M for English as combined ASR + `<EOU>` signal).
2. Text completeness: fast rules (trailing filler, conjunction, preposition or article → incomplete; digits or entities being dictated → extend; "wait / hold on / let me think" → long timeout) plus an optional end-of-turn probability from PAN's own 2B LLM, scoring the probability of an end-of-turn token after the partial transcript (LiveKit's method, applied to PAN's own model, so their license does not apply).
3. Fusion:
   - audio p(complete) ≥0.5 and text not incomplete → commit after ~200–300 ms total silence;
   - disagreement → re-check every ~200 ms and commit at ~0.8–1.2 s of silence if audio confidence holds;
   - both incomplete, or a trailing filler → wait up to `max_delay` 2–2.5 s, then commit (or give a soft prompt);
   - explicit "wait" → hold until the user resumes or says "go ahead".
4. Speculative LLM start at an eager point (audio p ≈0.3–0.4 after 200 ms silence); cancel if the user resumes; one speculation at a time; never play TTS before the commit.
5. Overlap classifier for continue / adapt / yield / ignore (section 8) on partial transcripts during playback.
6. Per-user adaptation: maintain the owner's within-turn pause distribution and false cut-off events; adjust `max_delay` (bounded) the way LiveKit's dynamic endpointing adapts to session pause statistics.
7. Metrics to log and tune against: false cut-off rate, median and P90 response gap, false barge-in rate, missed barge-ins, echo-triggered events. Smart Turn's test set, Krisp's `turn-taking-test-v1` and LiveKit's eot-bench data can serve as external checks, alongside PAN's own labelled recordings.

**Pendant (later, ESP32-S3)**
1. ESP-SR AFE "MR" (1 mic + playback reference = PCM sent to the I2S amp), AEC + VADNet on-device (~19% of one core, ~0.75 MB PSRAM in SR mode); "MMNR" if a second mic is added (~47–53% of a core, ~1.2 MB PSRAM) for BSS/beamforming, which also helps addressee detection.
2. Send AEC'd audio plus VAD flags to the phone; the phone keeps running Smart Turn, the barge-in gate and truncation tracking; the hub keeps the semantic layer.
3. Compute a near-field "wearer is speaking" feature on the pendant (energy/direct-to-reverberant ratio) to feed the addressee prior.
