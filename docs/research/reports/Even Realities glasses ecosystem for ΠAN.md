# Borrow Mentra's drivers, own the always-on gap

ΠAN can drive Even Realities G1 and G2 glasses from its own Android app today, with no Even app in the loop, by lifting the **Apache-2.0 Kotlin drivers in MentraOS** (`G1.kt`, `G2.kt`) and **Google's Apache-2.0 liblc3** decoder. Even's two official routes do not fit yet. The Even Hub SDK runs apps only as web pages inside Even's phone app, with no direct Bluetooth. The new Apache-2.0 Enterprise SDK is real native Kotlin, but it only talks to G2 units running enterprise firmware with a **signed licence per serial number**. Across the roughly **300 community projects**, almost everything is tap-to-talk feeding a cloud speech engine. Nobody ships always-on capture plus an addressed gate, voice ID and persistent memory, which is exactly ΠAN's design. The closest rivals are Work Bench (MIT, continuous capture and on-phone diarization, intent layer still "Planned"), cos-glasses-server (MIT, the most mature, but push-to-talk) and Even's own Conversate. For the Medallion, one setting settles the whole audio chain: **liblc3 at 16 kHz, 10 ms, 40-byte frames (32 kbps)**. That is the same format the G2 sends, so one decoder serves glasses and pendant on both phone and hub. Two things stay open: a CPU number for liblc3 on the XIAO ESP32-S3, and a patent question about LC3 used outside Bluetooth audio profiles.

## Three doors into the glasses, and only the unofficial one opens for ΠAN now

Even offers two official developer routes, and a third route exists through reverse engineering. Each has a different blocker.

**Door 1: Even Hub SDK.** Consumer G2 apps are web apps. "The phone runs the Even Realities App, which hosts your app in a WebView", and "The Even Realities App relays everything to and from the glasses over Bluetooth" ([Even Hub architecture](https://hub.evenrealities.com/docs/get-started/architecture)). The SDK `@evenrealities/even_hub_sdk` 0.0.16 and the simulator are **MIT**, but the `evenhub-cli` is **UNLICENSED** ([npm SDK](https://registry.npmjs.org/@evenrealities/even_hub_sdk/latest), [npm CLI](https://registry.npmjs.org/@evenrealities/evenhub-cli/latest)). Audio is simple: `bridge.audioControl(true, AudioInputSource.Glasses)` delivers 16 kHz signed 16-bit mono PCM from the "G2 four-mic array" once a startup page exists ([device-features skill](https://github.com/even-realities/everything-evenhub/blob/main/plugins/everything-evenhub/skills/device-features/SKILL.md)). Since 0.0.16, each frame carries a `speakerRole` tag (self, other, unknown), described as an "App algorithm result; this is not a firmware identity assertion" ([SDK types](https://unpkg.com/@evenrealities/even_hub_sdk@0.0.16/dist/index.d.ts)). The limits are hard for ΠAN. There is "No direct Bluetooth access", and no speech recognition method at all. The official `asr` template ships its STT client as a blank stub that throws ([asr stt.ts](https://github.com/even-realities/evenhub-templates/blob/main/asr/src/asr/stt.ts)). The network whitelist is a static list of exact origins fixed at pack time ([Cue README](https://github.com/tntpsu/Cue)). Apps only survive the background inside a headless WebView ([background-state skill](https://github.com/even-realities/everything-evenhub/blob/main/plugins/everything-evenhub/skills/background-state/SKILL.md)).

**Door 2: G2 Enterprise SDK.** Announced on 2026-11-10, it lets your own app connect "directly over Bluetooth" with "no need to install the Even Realities consumer app" ([Even blog](https://www.evenrealities.com/blogs/news/introducing-the-even-g2-enterprise-sdk)). The code is already public under **Apache-2.0** as `enterprise_sdk` (Kotlin) and `flutter_enterprise_sdk`. Release `0.1.0-beta.14` ships on Maven Central as `com.evenrealities.devices:even-devices-native-android`. It gives "built-in LC3 decoding and processed 16 kHz mono PCM16 audio callbacks", Even Hub page commands, input, IMU and OTA ([enterprise_sdk README](https://github.com/even-realities/enterprise_sdk)). The catch: "Firmware must expose the enterprise G2 service; a signed license for the exact glasses SN is required." G1, the R1 ring and "consumer G2 connection entry points" are out of scope ([Android guide](https://github.com/even-realities/enterprise_sdk/blob/main/docs/enterprise/enterprise-android-sdk.md)). Each developer account gets **three free device licences**. Licences never expire, but they "cannot be returned and devices cannot be removed" ([licences page](https://hub.evenrealities.com/docs/enterprise/licenses)). Whether a retail G2 can be enrolled is not stated anywhere. One more trap: the LC3 decoder ships as a precompiled `libeg3.so` whose "provenance and redistribution terms... still require confirmation from the rights holder" ([third-party notices](https://github.com/even-realities/enterprise_sdk/blob/main/native/licenses/ANDROID_THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)).

**Door 3: direct BLE on stock firmware.** The G1 protocol is officially documented in **EvenDemoApp (BSD-2-Clause)** ([EvenDemoApp](https://github.com/even-realities/EvenDemoApp)). The G2 protocol is reverse-engineered, but it works. MentraOS ships a production Kotlin driver. g2-kit-unofficial (MIT) has the best written explanation. men-g2-ble-gateway (MIT) "works on official firmware(v2.2.20)" with "No custom firmware required" ([men-g2-ble-gateway](https://github.com/gpsnmeajp/men-g2-ble-gateway)). The risks are real. Early g2-kit work "non-terminally bricked a pair by writing an unknown `dev_config` field" ([g2-kit gotchas](https://github.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/blob/main/ble/docs/gotchas.md)). Sources also disagree on the G2 header byte: `AA 21` in g2-kit's README and i-soxi's notes versus `AA 12` in Even-G2-RE ([i-soxi](https://github.com/i-soxi/even-g2-protocol), [Even-G2-RE](https://github.com/lonelyobserver0/Even-G2-RE)). Follow working code, not docs.

| Door | What ΠAN gets | Licence | Blocker | Fit for ΠAN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Even Hub SDK](https://hub.evenrealities.com/docs/get-started/overview) | 16 kHz PCM, `speakerRole`, text and image containers | SDK MIT; CLI UNLICENSED | WebView inside Even's app; no BLE; static whitelist; store review | Fallback only |
| [Enterprise SDK](https://github.com/even-realities/enterprise_sdk) | Native Kotlin, PCM16 callbacks, OTA, no Even app | Apache-2.0 (except `libeg3.so`) | Enterprise firmware plus per-serial licence; G2 only | Best if the owner's retail G2 qualifies |
| Direct BLE, stock firmware ([MentraOS G2.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/sgcs/G2.kt), [EvenDemoApp](https://github.com/even-realities/EvenDemoApp)) | Full control of G1 and G2, raw LC3, display, input | Apache-2.0 / BSD-2 / MIT | Reverse-engineered; firmware updates can break it | Recommended path now |

The two protocols differ a lot. The table below is the cheat sheet for ΠAN's driver.

| Item | G1 | G2 |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | Nordic UART `6E400001…`, two links, left arm first ([EvenDemoApp](https://github.com/even-realities/EvenDemoApp)) | EvenHub GATT `00002760-08C2-11E1-9073-0E8AC72E…`, write `5401`, notify `5402`, audio `6402` ([G2.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/sgcs/G2.kt)) |
| Framing | Raw command bytes | `0xAA` header, CRC16, protobuf payloads (hand-rolled in G2.kt) |
| Auth | None | Protobuf AuthMgr handshake, no crypto keys in G2.kt |
| Heartbeat | `0x25`; 8 s in EvenDemoApp, 15 s in MentraOS | About 5 s; the plugin task dies after about 10 s with no traffic ([gotchas](https://github.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/blob/main/ble/docs/gotchas.md)) |
| Mic on | `0x0E 0x01`, right arm only | Even Hub audio control; needs a live page; page rebuild kills audio |
| Mic data | `0xF1` + seq + LC3, 20-byte frames (16 kbps) | 40-byte LC3 frames (32 kbps), five per 205-byte notification ([g2-kit lc3-decoder.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/HEAD/ble/lc3-decoder.ts)) |
| Display | Text 488 px wide, 5 lines; 1-bit BMP 576×136 | 576×288, 16 greys; image containers about 200×100, tiled |
| Input | TouchBar events `0xF5` (MentraOS leaves touch disabled as "completely broken") | Click, double click, scroll, IMU; R1 ring through glasses or its own link |
| Official mic limit | "maximum supported recording duration is 30 seconds" in the Even AI flow | None documented; streams until stopped or heartbeat lapses ([g2-kit audio.md](https://github.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/blob/main/ble/docs/audio.md)) |

## MentraOS already wrote 10,000 lines of the driver ΠAN needs

MentraOS is the most active project in this space by far. It has **2,422 stars and 1,233 commits to `dev` in the 30 days before 2026-11-14**, with near-daily dev builds ([repo](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS), [commits](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/commits/dev)). Mentra Labs is a YC company that raised an **$8M seed in July 2025** ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/mentra-raises-8m-and-launches-mentraos-2-0-open-source-smartglasses-software/)). The repo says "Every component is open source under the Apache License 2.0" ([README](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/README.md)). One old mirror describes it as MIT, but the current LICENSE file is Apache-2.0, and that file wins ([LICENSE](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/LICENSE)).

The 2026 design matters for ΠAN. Apps are now local JS "miniapps" inside the Mentra phone app. Cloud V2 handles accounts and **Soniox-only** cloud speech recognition ([cloud-v2 README](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/cloud-v2/README.md), [soniox.ts](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/cloud-v2/packages/runtime/src/services/audio/providers/soniox.ts)). ΠAN needs none of that. **The glasses driver is phone-local.** Every device is a subclass of one abstract Kotlin class, `SGCManager`, with about 40 methods for text, bitmaps, mic, battery and connection ([SGCManager.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/sgcs/SGCManager.kt)). Glasses audio is decoded on the phone with liblc3 through JNI at 16 kHz and 10 ms. The PCM then fans out to a wake word spotter, Silero VAD, optional local sherpa-onnx STT and the cloud. The code comment reads "Audio always flows; nothing here gates it" ([DeviceManager.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/DeviceManager.kt)). That fan-out is the right shape for ΠAN's addressed gate.

**Lift the source; do not depend on the Maven artifact.** The published Bluetooth SDK sends usage analytics to Mentra's PostHog **on by default**. It also runs an update check against `core.mentraglass.com` **on every start, even with analytics off**. Analytics events include the glasses serial ([bluetooth-sdk README](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/README.md), [BluetoothSdkUpdateCheck.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/BluetoothSdkUpdateCheck.kt)). That clashes with ΠAN's privacy stance. Mentra's own docs also name only Mentra Live as supported by the standalone SDK ([Bluetooth SDK overview](https://docs.mentraglass.com/bluetooth-sdk/overview.md)). Copying the files avoids both problems.

| File to lift (under `mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/`) | Size | Purpose |
|---|---|---|

That is about **440 KB, or 10,500 lines**. The real porting work is the glue. G1.kt calls Mentra's `Bridge` 268 times and G2.kt 192 times; `DeviceStore` and `DeviceManager` add more. A thin shim can map those to ΠAN's logger, SharedPreferences and audio pipeline. After stripping dashboard menus, calendar widgets and OTA, the driver could shrink to about 5,000 to 7,000 lines. That is an estimate, not a measurement. Keep the Apache headers, add a NOTICE, pin a commit, and diff upstream on a schedule. Mentra's commit rate is effectively free maintenance against Even firmware changes.

Two cautions. First, the `lc3Lib` module also vendors **rnnoise and opus-tools**, whose licence files were not checked in the vendored copy. Drop them for decode-only use, or verify them first. Second, three repos in the same GitHub org have **no licence**: `OpenSourceSmartGlasses` (1,246 stars), `SmartGlassesManager_iOS` and `activelook-sdk` ([Mentra-Community org](https://github.com/Mentra-Community)). Do not use them. Also note what MentraOS lacks: **no speaker ID or voice enrolment**, only Soniox cloud diarization. Its speech stack is not better than what ΠAN already runs on its hub.

## A five-step Android path, with glasses as display first and mic second

The recommended path layers a ΠAN-owned driver on borrowed Apache code. It keeps the Enterprise SDK as an upgrade and Even Hub as a fallback.

| Step | What ΠAN does | Borrow from | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ask Even | The owner decides whether to ask Even's enterprise contact if a retail G2 can take one of the three free dev licences | [Enterprise licences](https://hub.evenrealities.com/docs/enterprise/licenses) | Yes or no on retail eligibility |
| 2. LC3 module | Add a Gradle module that builds Google liblc3 from source, LC3plus off; one decoder per source (G1 right arm, G2, Medallion) | [MentraOS lc3Lib](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/tree/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/lc3Lib), [liblc3](https://github.com/google/liblc3) | Decodes a recorded G2 capture to clean 16 kHz PCM |
| 3. Driver behind one interface | `GlassesDriver` with G1 and G2 classes lifted from MentraOS; shim for `Bridge`, `DeviceStore`, `DeviceManager`; telemetry removed | MentraOS (Apache-2.0); [g2-kit docs](https://github.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/tree/main/ble/docs) (MIT); [enterprise_sdk source](https://github.com/even-realities/enterprise_sdk) (Apache-2.0) as reference | Connect, auth, text on lens, mic frames in, taps out |
| 4. Always-on service | Android foreground service; heartbeat that never lapses; mic watchdog that re-enables after silence or reconnect; firmware version check at connect | Patterns from g2-kit and the enterprise guide | 8 hours connected with no silent mic drop |
| 5. Wire into ΠAN | Forward raw LC3 (about 4 KB/s) to the hub over Tailscale, or PCM into the phone's VAD and addressed gate; push replies as text containers | MentraOS fan-out pattern | A spoken question answered on the lens |

Several facts shape the details. On G2, "Never send audioControl(enable=true) without a live page", and page rebuilds kill the audio stream ([G2.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/sgcs/G2.kt)). So ΠAN must update containers in place while listening. BLE throughput limits redraws: the official template debounces to 120 ms, and other apps cap at one redraw per 0.5 s ([asr template](https://github.com/even-realities/evenhub-templates/tree/main/asr), [Soniox Note](https://github.com/kissyjpf/evenhub-sonioxnote)). If heartbeats stop, "mic frames stop" silently ([g2-kit audio.md](https://github.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/blob/main/ble/docs/audio.md)). On G1, the official 30-second cap belongs to the Even AI flow. MentraOS re-sends the mic command every 30 minutes, which suggests long sessions work, but this needs a hardware test ([G1.kt](https://github.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/blob/dev/mobile/modules/bluetooth-sdk/android/src/main/java/com/mentra/bluetoothsdk/sgcs/G1.kt)). MentraOS's G2 init also turns off "Hey Even" and skips onboarding. While ΠAN owns the link, Even's own AI, navigation and Conversate are gone. The enterprise guide's advice to check "whether another phone owns the glasses" points the same way ([Android guide](https://github.com/even-realities/enterprise_sdk/blob/main/docs/enterprise/enterprise-android-sdk.md)).

**Battery is the main unknown, and it changes the design.** The G2 has a **192 mAh** cell rated for "about two days of regular use" ([Even specs](https://support.evenrealities.com/hc/en-us/articles/13499229138959-Specs)). No one has published a measurement of continuous mic streaming. Phone-side VAD saves nothing on the glasses; only turning the mic off does. So ΠAN should keep the **Medallion as the always-on ear** and use the glasses mainly as a display. The glasses mic can serve as a second ear when the Medallion is absent or for noisy rooms. This is an inference from the specs, so measure it before deciding. One more loss on the direct path: `speakerRole` is computed by Even's app, so ΠAN's own driver will not get it. The Enterprise SDK does apply "speaker-role smoothing" in its native PCM processing, which is another reason to pursue step 1.

Avoid three shortcuts. Do not patch Even's app: even-g2-patched is MIT, but the APK it produces contains Even's code ([even-g2-patched](https://github.com/cokeeffekt/even-g2-patched)). Do not flash custom firmware: g2flash is GPL-3.0 and warns that flashing "will void your warranty" ([g2flash](https://github.com/jimrandomh/g2flash)). Do not write unknown `dev_config` fields. Even Hub stays as a fallback for users who want to keep Even's app. A small plugin built from the MIT `asr` template could stream PCM to the hub, but ΠAN's hub address would have to be a fixed whitelisted origin. Whether Tailscale names pass that check is untested.

## Some 300 projects, and none listens all day

The G2 awesome list holds about **305 entries in 16 sections**. A live pull resolved 285 repos and found **17 dead links**, about 6% gone within nine months ([awesome-even-realities-g2](https://github.com/pangoleen/awesome-even-realities-g2)). The G1 list has five entries and has been dead since December 2024 ([awesome-even-realities-g1](https://github.com/galfaroth/awesome-even-realities-g1)). Most projects are small single-feed web apps: **82 productivity apps**, about 30 navigation apps, 29 games. The real energy sits in about **40 AI and coding-agent bridges** and in reverse engineering. The voice section has only 12 entries, three of them dead. About 58 entries touch the mic somewhere. **None does continuous, background, local speech recognition on a phone or hub.** Outside MentraOS, only five community repos pass 100 stars, and none is a voice app.

The voice apps all follow one recipe: SDK PCM to a cloud engine. Soniox, Deepgram, OpenAI Whisper and Groq Whisper dominate. Local recognition appears only behind a user's own server: faster-whisper in TeleGlance and matrix-g2, speaches in even-matrix. The best published latency is Taraweeh Companion's **343 ms median** for Groq Whisper turbo, but that repo has no licence ([Taraweeh](https://github.com/wasimlhr/taraweeh-companion-g2)). even-voice-shim measures **1.0 to 1.4 s** end to end for short push-to-talk ([even-voice-shim](https://github.com/tntpsu/even-voice-shim)). Even claims its own captions arrive in "typically less than a second" ([Even blog](https://www.evenrealities.com/blog/ai-glasses-for-accessibility)). G2 Captions explicitly holds back from "always-on/background capture" ([G2 Captions](https://github.com/iamantonio/g2-captions)).

Addressee detection is where ΠAN is clearly ahead. The community state of the art is a system prompt or a wake word inside the transcript. The Unofficial Local Assistant runs always-on WhisperLive and tells its local Qwen model to "Stay silent unless addressed. False positives are worse than missed responses" ([soul.md](https://github.com/marienbaptiste/unofficial-even-g2-local-assistant/blob/HEAD/server/openclaw/soul.md)). Work Bench saves everything but only forwards turns that start with "hey" ([Work Bench](https://github.com/aaronrau/workbench)). Helix (G1) runs a heuristic first, then an LLM that returns **answer, ignore or wait** with a "kind" label and a 0.70 threshold ([PassiveTriggerClassifier.swift](https://github.com/FJiangArthur/Helix-iOS/blob/HEAD/NativeHelix/Sources/HelixConversation/PassiveTriggerClassifier.swift)). The "wait" state is worth adding to ΠAN's convbench labels. None of these projects publishes false-trigger or missed-address rates.

## One LC3 setting serves glasses, Medallion, phone and hub

**Use Google liblc3 1.1.3 (Apache-2.0) on every node.** It covers Bluetooth LC3 at 7.5 and 10 ms, 8 to 48 kHz, and 20 to 400 bytes per frame. It needs no dynamic memory, and its PLC runs when you pass a null frame ([lc3.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/liblc3/main/include/lc3.h)). It builds for the Android NDK with `LIBC=bionic`, for WASM with clang, and for Python ([liblc3 README](https://github.com/google/liblc3)). Build with `LC3_PLUS=0 LC3_PLUS_HR=0` to stay away from LC3plus, which is "not royalty-free" ([Wikipedia: LC3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LC3_(codec))). Espressif's `esp_audio_codec` 2.6.2 has a tuned LC3 with published ESP32-S3 numbers. Its licence, though, is "for use EXCLUSIVELY with Espressif Systems products" ([licence](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp_audio_codec/versions/2.6.2/license?language=en)). That is legal on the Medallion but useless on the phone and hub, and it would lock a future non-Espressif pendant out. Keep it only as a speed fallback.

Each node needs its own build. Android has **no public MediaCodec LC3**; the formats table lists Opus but not LC3 ([Android formats](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/supported-formats)). So the app bundles liblc3 as a JNI library, exactly as MentraOS does. The hub is a Windows mini-PC, and PyPI's `lc3py` ships wheels **only for Linux x86-64 and macOS ARM64** ([lc3py](https://pypi.org/project/lc3py/)). The cleanest fit for ΠAN's Node server is MentraOS's `liblc3.wasm` worker: 10 ms, 16 kHz, 160 samples, one decoder state per stream ([lc3.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Mentra-Community/MentraOS/dev/cloud-v2/packages/runtime/src/services/audio/workers/lc3.ts)). The other option is to build lc3py from source on Windows.

**Pick 16 kHz, 10 ms, 40 bytes per frame: 32 kbps.** This is the Bluetooth SIG's wideband voice point. In SIG listening tests, LC3 there "outperformed G.722 and mSBC... even though G.722 and mSBC operate at approximately twice the bitrate", and 7.5 ms and 10 ms frames scored the same ([SIG white paper](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/LC3Characterization_WP.pdf)). At 16 kbps, LC3 scored lowest in the OpenACE benchmark, so avoid the minimum ([OpenACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08374v2)). The G2 uses this same setting: five 40-byte frames per 205-byte notification, about 32.8 kbps on air ([g2-kit lc3-decoder.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Commute773/g2-kit-unofficial/HEAD/ble/lc3-decoder.ts)). If the Medallion copies that packing, **ΠAN runs one decoder config for G2 and Medallion.** The bandwidth case is strong too. LC3 at 32 kbps is 4 KB/s, or 20 notifications per second. Raw 16 kHz PCM would need about 131 full notifications per second. Real Android links managed 84 to 128 per second in one Nordic test ([Nordic DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/85258/comunicating-at-least-300-kbp-s-via-ble-nrf5340-with-android-phones)).

CPU looks fine but is unproven. Espressif's own LC3 encoder used **46.57% CPU and 3.67 KB** for 48 kHz stereo on an ESP32-S3R8 ([esp_audio_codec readme](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp_audio_codec/versions/2.6.2/readme?language=en)). 16 kHz mono is a sixth of those samples, which projects to roughly 5 to 10% of a core. liblc3 is generic float C with ARM optimisations but nothing for Xtensa, so it may be slower ([liblc3 src](https://github.com/google/liblc3/tree/main/src)). From its memory macros, encoder state is about 2.6 KB and decoder state about 3.1 KB at 16 kHz mono ([lc3_private.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/liblc3/main/include/lc3_private.h)). For comparison, Opus at 16 kHz on the same chip ran at a real-time factor of 0.18 to 0.68 ([micro-opus benchmark](https://components.espressif.com/components/esphome/micro-opus/versions/0.4.1/examples/encode_benchmark)). No one has published liblc3 numbers on an ESP32, so ΠAN has to measure.

| Node | Build | Settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medallion (XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense) | liblc3 as an ESP-IDF component, `-O2`, LC3plus off; static encoder in internal RAM | 16 kHz, 10 ms, 40 B | Packet `[seq][flags][5 × 40 B]` = 202 B every 50 ms; MTU 247, 2M PHY, DLE 251, CI 30 to 50 ms; send only while VAD is open, with about 300 ms pre-roll |
| Android app | JNI module like MentraOS `lc3Lib` | One decoder per source: Medallion, G1 right arm (20 B), G2 (40 B) | On a seq gap, call decode with null once per lost frame, cap about 10, then reset; mark the gap for the ASR |
| Hub (Windows, Node) | liblc3 WASM worker, or lc3py built from source | 16 kHz Int16 out | Phone forwards raw LC3 over Tailscale (4 KB/s) to save bandwidth |

Patents are the one soft spot. Fraunhofer says LC3 is licensed "exclusively for use according to the Bluetooth Special Interest Group (Bluetooth SIG) audio profiles" ([Fraunhofer IIS](https://www.iis.fraunhofer.de/en/ff/amm/lizenz/patent.html)). A Medallion streaming LC3 over a custom GATT characteristic, then over Wi-Fi to a hub, is not an LE Audio profile. Even Realities and MentraOS ship exactly this pattern, so the practical risk looks low. Still, before a commercial launch, ΠAN should join the Bluetooth SIG at the free Adopter level, qualify the Medallion, and ask Fraunhofer's LC3 licensing contact whether a proprietary GATT stream needs a licence. Opus (BSD) has no such question, which makes it the fallback codec. LC3 decoding is needed for Even glasses either way.

### The test to run on the XIAO ESP32-S3

The pass marks below are proposed targets, not published figures.

| # | Test | How | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | liblc3 encode speed | Encode 1,000 frames of real PDM mic audio at 16 kHz, 10 ms, 40 B on core 1; time each with `esp_timer_get_time()` | Mean under 1 ms per frame (under 10% of a core); worst case under 2 ms |
| 2 | Same with Espressif codec | Repeat test 1 with `esp_audio_codec` | Decides fallback only; keep liblc3 if test 1 passes |
| 3 | Memory | Check the encoder task's stack high-water mark; keep codec state in internal RAM, not PSRAM | Stack use inside a 4 to 8 KB budget |
| 4 | Real load | Run encode plus VAD plus BLE streaming together for 30 minutes to a Pixel | 20 notifications per second; no seq gaps; no watchdog resets |
| 5 | Speech accuracy | On the hub, pass a speech set through LC3 at 16, 24 and 32 kbps, Opus at the same rates, and raw PCM; score WER with ΠAN's Parakeet or Whisper | LC3 32 kbps within about half a point of raw PCM WER |
| 6 | Radio duty | Compare current draw with VAD-gated sending against always sending | Confirms the battery win before the design is locked |

If test 1 fails, try the Espressif codec on the Medallion. If test 5 shows LC3 hurting WER, move to Opus SILK at low complexity, which stays near a 0.2 real-time factor.

## Conclusion

The glasses driver is a commodity now. Between Mentra's Apache code, Even's own Apache Enterprise SDK and g2-kit's MIT notes, connecting to G1 or G2 is a porting job, not research. What nobody has is the part ΠAN already builds: hearing everything, deciding when it is being spoken to, knowing who is speaking, and remembering it across days on hardware the user owns. The ecosystem's habits confirm the gap. Even's platform pushes developers toward tap-to-talk and cloud engines. The one project with a matching architecture, Work Bench, has not built its intent layer. ΠAN should spend its effort on the gate, voice ID and memory, and treat the glasses as a borrowed screen.

The codec choice ties the hardware story together in a way that was not obvious before. Because the G2 already streams 32 kbps LC3 in five-frame packets, a Medallion built the same way gives ΠAN one decoder and one pipeline for every wearable it supports. The real risks sit outside the code. Even could change consumer firmware, or decide whether retail units can join the enterprise path. Fraunhofer's view of LC3 outside Bluetooth profiles is unknown. Always-on mic drain on a 192 mAh cell has never been measured. Each of these is cheap to settle now: one email to Even, one to Fraunhofer, and one afternoon of tests on the XIAO and a pair of glasses.
